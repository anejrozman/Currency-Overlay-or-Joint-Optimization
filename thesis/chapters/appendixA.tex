\chapter{Optimization Derivation: Expected Shortfall and Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:es_derivation}

In this appendix, we derive the portfolio optimization problems used in the main text, building up from the definitions of
Expected Shortfall and second-order Expected Shortfall to the programs solved in the empirical analysis.
In \Cref{app:es_auxiliary_formulation} we derive the auxiliary formulation of
Expected Shortfall and introduce second-order Expected Shortfall. In \Cref{app:linear-program-representation} we write the portfolio optimization problem as a linear program for Expected Shortfall
and as a second-order cone program for second-order Expected Shortfall. In \Cref{app:regularization} we regularize the programs by adding an $L^1$
penalty, an $L^2$ penalty, and a leverage constraint. We
then extend the programs to the multi-currency setting, for sequential optimization in \Cref{app:separate-optimization}
and for joint optimization in \Cref{app:joint-optimization}. Finally, in \Cref{app:mean-risk} we add the expected
portfolio return to the objectives of the sequential and joint problems.

\section{Auxiliary Formulations of Expected Shortfall and Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:es_auxiliary_formulation}

We derive the equivalence between the direct minimization of Expected Shortfall and the auxiliary formulation proposed
by Rockafellar and Uryasev \cite{RockafellarUryasev2000}, and introduce second-order Expected Shortfall, which Krokhmal
\cite{Krokhmal2007} defines directly through an auxiliary formulation of the same type. The two formulations
allow for writing the portfolio optimization problem as a linear program for Expected Shortfall and as a second-order cone
program for second-order Expected Shortfall in \Cref{app:linear-program-representation}.

\subsection{Expected Shortfall} \label{app:es_auxiliary}

We assume that the loss distribution is continuous and has a strictly increasing cumulative distribution function and additionally that its probability density
function exists. Under these assumptions, the Value-at-Risk (VaR) defined below is the unique point at which the
cumulative distribution function equals the confidence level \cite{QuantitativeRiskManagement2016}. The assumptions
simplify the derivation but are not needed for the result. Rockafellar and Uryasev \cite{RockafellarUryasev2002} show
that the equivalence derived below holds for general loss distributions, including the empirical distribution used in
\Cref{app:linear-program-representation}, when Expected Shortfall is defined as the average of the quantiles of the loss
distribution over all levels above the confidence level \cite{acerbi2002coherenceexpectedshortfall}.

\newpage
Define the loss random variable at time $t+1$, given portfolio weights $\bm{x}_t\in\mathbb{R}^N$, as $L(\bm{x}_t) = -\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1}$, where $\bm{R}_{t+1}\in\mathbb{R}^N$ is the asset return vector. Let $f(l)$ denote the probability density function of the loss $L(\bm{x}_t)$.\ The VaR at confidence level $\beta$ is defined as the $\beta$-quantile of the loss distribution
\begin{equation*}
    \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) = \min \{ \alpha : \mathbb{P}_t(L(\bm{x}_t) \le \alpha) \ge \beta \}.
\end{equation*}

The Expected Shortfall at confidence level $\beta$ is defined as the conditional expectation of losses exceeding $\text{VaR}_\beta$
\begin{equation*}
    \text{ES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) = \mathbb{E}_t[L(\bm{x}_t) \mid L(\bm{x}_t) \ge \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)] = \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)}^\infty l f(l) \, dl.
\end{equation*}

We now consider the auxiliary function $F_\beta$ defined as
\begin{align}
    F_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha) &= \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{E}_t[(L(\bm{x}_t) - \alpha)^+] \nonumber \\ 
    &= \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_\alpha^\infty (l - \alpha) f(l) \, dl. \label{eq:aux-function-es}
\end{align}
 
Before minimizing \eqref{eq:aux-function-es} with respect to $\alpha$, we verify convexity. Using Leibniz's rule, the first and second derivatives of $F_\beta$ with respect to $\alpha$ are
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial F_\beta}{\partial \alpha}
        &= 1 + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \frac{\partial}{\partial \alpha} \int_\alpha^\infty (l - \alpha) f(l) \, dl \\
    &= 1 + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \left[ -f(\alpha) \cdot 0 + \int_\alpha^\infty (-1) f(l) \, dl \right] \\
    &= 1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\alpha}^\infty f(l) \, dl, \\
    \frac{\partial^2 F_\beta}{\partial \alpha^2} &= \frac{1}{1-\beta} f(\alpha).
\end{align*}
Since the probability density function $f(\alpha) \ge 0$ and $\beta < 1$, the second derivative is non-negative. Thus, \eqref{eq:aux-function-es}  is convex in $\alpha$, ensuring that the first-order condition is a necessary and sufficient condition for a global minimum.


Setting the derivative to zero gives the optimal $\alpha^*$
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial F_\beta}{\partial \alpha} &= 0 \\
    1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\alpha^*}^\infty f(l) \, dl &= 0 \\
    1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{P}_t(L(\bm{x}_t) \ge \alpha^*) &= 0 \\
    \mathbb{P}_t(L(\bm{x}_t) \ge \alpha^*) &= 1 - \beta \\
    \mathbb{P}_t(L(\bm{x}_t) \le \alpha^*) &= \beta.
\end{align*}

We see that the optimal $\alpha^*$ that minimizes the auxiliary function for a fixed $\bm{x}_t$ is exactly the Value-at-Risk at level $\beta$.

Substituting $\alpha^* = \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)$ back into \eqref{eq:aux-function-es} we obtain
\begin{align*}
    F_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)) &= \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)}^\infty (l - \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)) f(l) \, dl \\
    &= \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \left( \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)}^\infty l f(l) \, dl - \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)}^\infty f(l) \, dl \right).
\end{align*}

Recognizing that $\int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)}^\infty f(l) \, dl = 1-\beta$, we have
\begin{align*}
    F_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)) &= \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)}^\infty l f(l) \, dl - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) (1-\beta) \\
    &= \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) + \text{ES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) - \text{VaR}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) \\
    &= \text{ES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t).
\end{align*}

Thus, minimizing $\text{ES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)$ with respect to $\bm{x}_t$ is equivalent to minimizing $F_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$ jointly with respect to $\bm{x}_t$ and $\alpha$
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_opt}
    \min_{\bm{x}_t} \text{ES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) = \min_{\bm{x}_t, \alpha} \left( \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{E}_t[(-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)^+] \right).
\end{equation}

Finally, we show that $F_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$ is jointly convex in $(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$. The inner map $(\bm{x}_t, \alpha) \mapsto (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)$ is affine and the function $z \mapsto z^+$ is convex and nondecreasing. The composition of a convex function with an affine map is convex \cite[Section~3.2.2]{boyd2004convex}. Thus, $(-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)^+$ is jointly convex in $(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$. Expectation is a linear operator which preserves convexity, and the sum of convex functions is convex.


\subsection{Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:soes_auxiliary}

Second-order Expected Shortfall belongs to the family of higher moment risk measures introduced by Krokhmal
\cite{Krokhmal2007}. It is obtained from the auxiliary function $F_\beta$ by replacing the expectation of the excess
loss $(L(\bm{x}_t) - \alpha)^+$ with its $L^2$ norm.\footnote{ We define the $L^2$ norm in the standard way as $\|Z\|_{L^2} = (\mathbb{E}_t[Z^2])^{1/2}$ for a random variable
$Z$.} This gives the auxiliary function
\begin{equation*}
    G_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha) = \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \big\| (L(\bm{x}_t) - \alpha)^+ \big\|_{L^2},
\end{equation*}
and second-order Expected Shortfall at confidence level $\beta$ is defined as its minimum over $\alpha$
\begin{equation} \label{eq:soes_def}
    \text{SOES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) = \min_{\alpha} G_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha).
\end{equation}
The $L^2$ norm squares the excess losses, so large losses 
receive more weight than in Expected Shortfall. Second-order Expected Shortfall is a coherent risk measure, which we prove in Proposition~\ref{prop:soes-coherent}.

Unlike Expected Shortfall, second-order Expected Shortfall is defined directly through the minimization over $\alpha$,
so there is no equivalence to derive. Instead, we only have to verify that $G_\beta$ is jointly convex in $(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$.  

We argued for the case of $F_\beta$ that the map $(\bm{x}_t, \alpha) \mapsto (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)^+$ is convex. The $L^2$ norm is a convex function \cite[Section~3.1.5]{boyd2004convex} and nondecreasing for non-negative random variables.  The composition of a convex nondecreasing function with a convex
function is again convex \cite[Section~3.2.4]{boyd2004convex}. Hence $\big\| (L(\bm{x}_t) - \alpha)^+ \big\|_{L^2} $ is jointly convex in $(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$, and the sum of convex functions is convex. 

By definition \eqref{eq:soes_def}, minimizing $\text{SOES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t)$ with respect to $\bm{x}_t$ is
equivalent to minimizing $G_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$ jointly with respect to $\bm{x}_t$ and $\alpha$
\begin{equation} \label{eq:soes_joint_opt}
    \min_{\bm{x}_t} \text{SOES}_\beta(\bm{x}_t) = \min_{\bm{x}_t, \alpha} \left( \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta}
    \big\| (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)^+ \big\|_{L^2} \right).
\end{equation}

Since both $F_\beta$ and $G_\beta$ are convex, any local minimum is global, ensuring that
both optimization problems can be solved reliably with standard convex optimization methods.

\section{Representation for Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:linear-program-representation}
 
Let $\rho_\beta$ denote either of the two risk measures, written as a
function of the portfolio return $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1}$. The portfolio optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:risk_min}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \rho_\beta\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr), \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where the budget constraint enforces full investment. By \eqref{eq:joint_opt} and \eqref{eq:soes_joint_opt}, problem
\eqref{eq:risk_min} is equivalent to minimizing $F_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$ or $G_\beta(\bm{x}_t, \alpha)$ jointly
with respect to $\bm{x}_t$ and $\alpha$.
Each program in this appendix defines its own decision vector, vector of constants, and penalty and constraint matrices, so
the same symbols denote slightly different objects in different programs, and we refer to a specific program by its equation
number when discussing them. 


\subsection{Expected Shortfall} \label{app:lp_es}

Let $\bm{y}_1, \dots, \bm{y}_q \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote $q$ historical realizations of the asset return vector
$\bm{R}_{t+1}$. We approximate the conditional expectation in $F_\beta$ by the empirical average over this sample
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}_t[(-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)^+]
    \approx \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+,
\end{equation*}
so that the portfolio optimization problem under Expected Shortfall becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:empirical_es}
    \bm{x}_t^*, \alpha^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t, \alpha} \left\{ \alpha
    + \frac{1}{q(1-\beta)} \sum_{k=1}^q (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+ \right\},
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1.
\end{equation}
The summand $(-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+$ is convex but non-linear. We linearize it by introducing, for each
observation $k$, an auxiliary variable $u_k \ge 0$ subject to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:slack_constraint}
    u_k \ge -\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k + \alpha + u_k \ge 0.
\end{equation}
Replacing each summand by $u_k$, the objective becomes $\alpha + \frac{1}{q(1-\beta)} \sum_{k=1}^q u_k$. It is
strictly increasing in each $u_k$ and minimized, so every $u_k$ is driven to its lower bound at the optimum,
$u_k^* = (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+$. 

By collecting the decision variables in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} x_{1,t}, \dots, x_{N,t}, &  \alpha, & u_1, \dots, u_q \end{bmatrix}^\top
    = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top, & \alpha, & \bm{u}^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q+1},
\end{equation*}
and constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{c} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top, &  1, & \dfrac{1}{q(1-\beta)} \bm{1}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q+1},
\end{equation*}
the portfolio optimization problem \eqref{eq:empirical_es} can be written as a linear program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:base_lp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* = \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
     \bm{c}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \bm{D} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_{q+1}^\top \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = E = 1, \\[2pt]
    & \bm{A} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \le \bm{b} = \bm{0}_q, \\[2pt]
    & u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (N+q+1)}$ encodes the budget constraint, while
$\bm{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+q+1)}$ and $\bm{b} \in \mathbb{R}^{q}$ encode the
$q$ inequality constraints arising from the linearization \eqref{eq:slack_constraint}. The matrix
$\bm{R}^{\text{data}} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ is composed of the sampled return vectors $\bm{y}_k^\top$ row by
row, $\bm{0}_q$ is the $q$-dimensional zero vector, and $\bm{I}_q$ the $q$-dimensional identity matrix.

Problem \eqref{eq:base_lp} has a linear objective subject to one linear equality and $q$
linear inequalities, and is thus a linear program solvable with standard methods. This
formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under Expected Shortfall.

\subsection{Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:socp_soes}

Using the same historical realizations $\bm{y}_1, \dots, \bm{y}_q$ of \Cref{app:lp_es}, we approximate the $L^2$ norm in $G_\beta$ by its
empirical average over the sample
\begin{equation*}
    \big\| (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} - \alpha)^+ \big\|_{L^2}
    \approx \left( \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \big( (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+ \big)^2 \right)^{1/2},
\end{equation*}
so that the portfolio optimization problem under second-order Expected Shortfall becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:empirical_soes}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{x}_t^*, \alpha^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t, \alpha} \left\{ \alpha
    + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \left( \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \big( (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+ \big)^2 \right)^{1/2}
    \right\}, \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
As in \Cref{app:lp_es}, we replace each term $(-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+$ by $u_k$ subject to
\eqref{eq:slack_constraint}. The empirical norm then becomes
$\big( \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q u_k^2 \big)^{1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{q}} \|\bm{u}\|_2$, and the objective becomes
$\alpha + \frac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} \|\bm{u}\|_2$. The Euclidean norm is strictly increasing in
every positive $u_k$, so, as for Expected Shortfall, every $u_k$ is driven to its lower bound at the optimum,
$u_k^* = (-\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k - \alpha)^+$.

The objective is still not linear, so we introduce one more auxiliary variable $s$ subject to the constraint
$\|\bm{u}\|_2 \le s$ and minimize $\alpha + \frac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} s$ instead. Since $\frac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} > 0$, $s^* = \|\bm{u}^*\|_2$ at the optimum, and the objective is linear in the decision variables. The constraint
$\|\bm{u}\|_2 \le s$ requires the vector $(\bm{u}, s) \in \mathbb{R}^{q+1}$ to lie in the second-order cone, the set of
vectors whose last entry is at least the Euclidean norm of the remaining entries \cite[Section~2.2.3]{boyd2004convex}.
This set is a cone, since it contains every non-negative multiple of its elements, and it is convex, so the constraint
keeps the optimization problem convex. An optimization problem with a linear objective, linear constraints, and
second-order cone constraints is a second-order cone program \cite[Section~4.4.2]{boyd2004convex}.

By collecting the decision variables in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top, & \alpha, & \bm{u}^\top, & s \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q+2},
\end{equation*}
and constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{c} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top, &  1, & \bm{0}_q^\top, & \dfrac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q+2},
\end{equation*}
the portfolio optimization problem \eqref{eq:empirical_soes} can be written as a second-order cone program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:base_socp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* = \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
     \bm{c}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \bm{D} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_{q+2}^\top \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = E = 1, \\[2pt]
    & \bm{A} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \le \bm{b} = \bm{0}_q, \\[2pt]
    & \|\bm{u}\|_2 \le s, \\[2pt]
    & u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (N+q+2)}$ and $\bm{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+q+2)}$ are the constraint
matrices of \eqref{eq:base_lp} with a zero column added for the variable $s$. This formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under
second-order Expected Shortfall.

\section{Regularization} \label{app:regularization}

The base problems \eqref{eq:base_lp} and \eqref{eq:base_socp} are known to be numerically unstable in
high-dimensional settings, producing extreme and concentrated weights \citep{ciliberti2007feasibility, kondor2007noise}.
We stabilize them with three regularization techniques, an $L^1$-norm penalty that promotes sparsity, an $L^2$-norm
penalty that promotes shrinkage, and an asset leverage constraint that bounds gross exposure. Each is appended to the base
problems while keeping them convex. 

\subsection{$L^1$ Regularization} \label{app:l1}

The $L^1$ penalty $\lambda_1 \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 = \lambda_1 \sum_{i=1}^N |x_{i,t}|$, with
$\lambda_1 \ge 0$, is non-linear through the absolute value. Following \cite{roncalli2013introduction},
we linearize it by splitting each weight into its positive and negative parts via the
auxiliary variables $\Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0$ and $\Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0$
\begin{equation} \label{eq:l1_decomp}
    x_{i,t} = \Delta_{i,t}^+ - \Delta_{i,t}^-,
    \qquad
    |x_{i,t}| = \Delta_{i,t}^+ + \Delta_{i,t}^-.
\end{equation}
The second identity holds at the optimum. Minimizing the $L^1$ penalty drives the $\Delta_{i,t}^+$ and $\Delta_{i,t}^-$ to
their smallest feasible values, so at most one is
non-zero, giving $\Delta_{i,t}^+ = \max(x_{i,t}, 0)$ and
$\Delta_{i,t}^- = \max(-x_{i,t}, 0)$. Writing the positive and negative parts as
vectors
$\bm{\Delta}_t^+ = [\Delta_{1,t}^+, \dots, \Delta_{N,t}^+]^\top$ and
$\bm{\Delta}_t^- = [\Delta_{1,t}^-, \dots, \Delta_{N,t}^-]^\top$, the $L^1$ penalty is written as
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda_1 \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 = \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-).
\end{equation*}
We append $\bm{\Delta}_t^-$ and $\bm{\Delta}_t^+$ to the decision vector, with the penalty coefficient $\lambda_1$ on
each of their entries in the vector of constants. The decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} is enforced as $N$ additional linear
equality constraints, $\bm{x}_t + \bm{\Delta}_t^- - \bm{\Delta}_t^+ = \bm{0}_N$, alongside the original budget
constraint.

\subsection{$L^2$ Regularization} \label{app:l2}

The $L^2$ penalty $\lambda_2 \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 = \lambda_2 \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{x}_t$, with
$\lambda_2 \ge 0$, is quadratic in the weights and is written as a quadratic form
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda_2 \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{Q}\, \bm{x}_t,
    \qquad \bm{Q} = \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N.
\end{equation*}
Embedding this in the decision vector $\tilde{\bm{x}}_t$, the penalty becomes
$\tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t$, where $\tilde{\bm{Q}}$ places the
block $\lambda_2 \bm{I}_N$ on the asset weights and zeros for the rest of the variables. Adding this term to the linear
objective of the base problems makes the objective quadratic, and since $\tilde{\bm{Q}}$ is positive semi-definite, it
remains convex.

\subsection{Leverage Constraint} \label{app:leverage}

The leverage constraint $\|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a$, with $\ell^a \ge 1$, bounds the portfolio's
gross exposure and guarantees a bounded feasible region even when $\lambda_1 = 0$. Reusing
the decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp}, it is expressed as a single linear inequality
\begin{equation} \label{eq:leverage_constraint}
    \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 = \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-) \le \ell^a.
\end{equation}
The choice $\ell^a = 1$ rules out leverage entirely and is equivalent to a hard $L^1$
constraint.

\subsection{Expected Shortfall} \label{app:reg_qp}

Adding all three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:base_lp} extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top,& \alpha,& \bm{u}^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q+1},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top,& 1,& \tfrac{1}{q(1-\beta)} \bm{1}_q^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times (2N+q+1)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+q+1) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+q+1) \times (2N+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The portfolio optimization problem under Expected Shortfall then becomes a quadratic program with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:reg_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t +  \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_{q+1}^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top \\
    \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times (q+1)} & \bm{I}_N & -\bm{I}_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \bm{0}_{q \times N} \\
    \bm{0}_N^\top & 0 & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     & \quad u_k \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforces the budget constraint and the remaining $N$ rows
enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp}. The first $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$
encode the inequality constraints from the linearization \eqref{eq:slack_constraint}, extended with zeros for the new
variables, and the final row of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ imposes the leverage constraint \eqref{eq:leverage_constraint}.

\subsection{Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:reg_socp}

Adding the same three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:base_socp} extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top,& \alpha,& \bm{u}^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^+)^\top,& s \end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q+2},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top,& 1,& \bm{0}_q^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \tfrac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times (2N+q+2)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+q+2) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+q+2) \times (2N+q+2)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The portfolio optimization problem under second-order Expected Shortfall then becomes a second-order cone program
with a convex quadratic objective
\begin{equation} \label{eq:reg_socp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t + \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_{q+1}^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & 0 \\
    \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times (q+1)} & \bm{I}_N & -\bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \bm{0}_q \\
    \bm{0}_N^\top & 0 & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & 0 \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \|\bm{u}\|_2 &\le s, \\[2pt]
     & \quad u_k \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ and $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ are the constraint matrices of \eqref{eq:reg_qp} with a zero column added
for the variable $s$, and the cone constraint is the same as in \eqref{eq:base_socp}.

\section{Sequential Optimization} \label{app:separate-optimization}
So far, we have formulated the portfolio optimization problem over the asset weights only. An international portfolio,
however, holds assets and currency positions as well, so constructing it
also requires selecting currency exposures. In the sequential portfolio optimization approach, the asset weights and currency exposures are optimized in two steps. First, the
asset weights $\bm{x}_t$ are optimized on the fully hedged returns. Then, conditional on the optimal
$\bm{x}_t^*$, the currency exposures $\bm{\psi}_t \in \mathbb{R}^M$ are optimized. The two optimization steps are given
by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sep_asset_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \rho_\beta\biggl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} \biggr)
    + \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,\ \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sep_curr_obj}
    \bm{\psi}_t^* \mid \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\psi}_t} \left\{
    \rho_\beta\biggl( \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t) \biggr)
    + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The regularization parameters are kept separate across assets ($\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$)
and currencies ($\lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c$).

The fully hedged return vector $\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}$ (defined in \Cref{sec:matrix-representation}) takes the place of the asset return vector $\bm{R}_{t+1}$ of
Sections~\ref{app:es_auxiliary_formulation}--\ref{app:regularization}, so the asset step \eqref{eq:sep_asset_obj} is
identical to the regularized programs \eqref{eq:reg_qp} for Expected Shortfall and
\eqref{eq:reg_socp} for second-order Expected Shortfall, and is obtained from them by
substituting the matrix of historically sampled fully hedged returns $\bm{R}^{fh}\in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ for
$\bm{R}^{\text{data}}$ and the asset-specific penalties $\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$ for $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$.

In the currency step \eqref{eq:sep_curr_obj}, the asset weights $\bm{x}_t^*$ are fixed and the currency exposures
$\bm{\psi}_t$ are optimized. The currency exposure $\psi_{c,t}$ is the net exposure of the portfolio to the foreign currency $c$ as defined in
\Cref{sec:matrix-representation}, and each unit of net exposure earns the excess currency return
$e_{c,t+1} - f_{c,t}$. Using the same auxiliary
representation for the risk measures and the empirical approximation as in \Cref{app:linear-program-representation}, we introduce $\alpha_\psi$ and auxiliary variables $u_{k,\psi} \ge 0$, and the slack constraint
\eqref{eq:slack_constraint} becomes
\begin{equation*}
    u_{k,\psi} \ge -\bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_k - \bm{f}_k) - \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_k - \alpha_\psi
    \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
    -\bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_k - \bm{f}_k) - \alpha_\psi - u_{k,\psi} \le \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_k,
\end{equation*}
where $\bm{R}^{fh}_k$ is the $k$-th sampled fully hedged return vector, and $\bm{e}_k$ and $\bm{f}_k$ are the $k$-th
sampled currency return and forward premium vectors. We stack the sampled excess currency returns $(\bm{e}_k - \bm{f}_k)^\top$ row by row into the matrix
$\bm{R}^c \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times M}$ and collect the fixed asset-portfolio returns in
$\bm{R}^{fh}_P = \bm{R}^{fh} \bm{x}_t^* \in \mathbb{R}^q$.

The currency step has no budget constraint, since the currency overlay is a zero-investment portfolio whose domestic
exposure $\psi_{0,t} = -\bm{1}_M^\top \bm{\psi}_t$ is inferred residually, and no leverage constraint is imposed on the
currency exposures. We linearize the currency $L^1$ penalty with the decomposition
$\psi_{c,t} = \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ - \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^-$ as in \Cref{app:l1} and write the currency $L^2$ penalty as
a quadratic form with the block $\lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M$ as in \Cref{app:l2}.

\subsection{Currency Overlay under Expected Shortfall} \label{app:sep_curr_es}

For Expected Shortfall, the decision vector of the currency step is
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \alpha_\psi,& \bm{u}_\psi^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3M+q+1},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_M^\top,& 1,& \tfrac{1}{q(1-\beta)} \bm{1}_q^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times (2M+q+1)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2M+q+1) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2M+q+1) \times (2M+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The currency overlay optimization under Expected Shortfall then becomes a quadratic program with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sep_curr_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times (q+1)} & \bm{I}_M & -\bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \bm{0}_M, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^c & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times M} & \bm{0}_{q \times M} \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \bm{R}^{fh}_P, \\[4pt]
    & \quad u_{k,\psi} \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } c = 1, \dots, M,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $M$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} for the currency exposures,
and the $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ encode the slack constraints of the currency step, with the fixed
asset-portfolio returns $\bm{R}^{fh}_P$ on the right-hand side.

\subsection{Currency Overlay under Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:sep_curr_soes}

For second-order Expected Shortfall, we introduce, as in \Cref{app:socp_soes}, one more auxiliary variable $s_\psi$
subject to the cone constraint $\|\bm{u}_\psi\|_2 \le s_\psi$, which extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \alpha_\psi,& \bm{u}_\psi^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top,& s_\psi \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3M+q+2},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_M^\top,& 1,& \bm{0}_q^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,& \tfrac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times (2M+q+2)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2M+q+2) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2M+q+2) \times (2M+q+2)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The currency overlay optimization under second-order Expected Shortfall then becomes a second-order cone program with
a convex quadratic objective
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sep_curr_socp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times (q+1)} & \bm{I}_M & -\bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_M \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \bm{0}_M, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^c & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times M} & \bm{0}_{q \times M} & \bm{0}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \bm{R}^{fh}_P, \\[4pt]
    \|\bm{u}_\psi\|_2 &\le s_\psi, \\[2pt]
    & \quad u_{k,\psi} \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } c = 1, \dots, M,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ and $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ are the constraint matrices of \eqref{eq:sep_curr_qp} with a zero column
added for the variable $s_\psi$.

\section{Joint Optimization} \label{app:joint-optimization}

In the joint portfolio optimization approach, the asset weights and currency exposures are optimized
simultaneously rather than sequentially. We stack them into the combined decision vector
$\bm{\theta}_t = [\bm{x}_t^\top, \bm{\psi}_t^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$ and the corresponding returns into the
combined return vector
$\bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} = [\bm{R}^{fh\top}_{t+1},\, (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t)^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$,
so that $\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}
+ \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t)$ is the hedged portfolio return of \Cref{app:separate-optimization}.
The joint optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{\theta}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\theta}_t} \left\{ \rho_\beta\biggl( \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} \biggr)
    + \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 + \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = 1, \quad \|\bm{L}^a \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The following matrices and vector adapt the problem to the
joint optimization setting
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{q}_{N,M} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N \\ \bm{0}_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{L}^a = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \bm{0}_{M \times M} \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{S}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^a \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \lambda_1^c \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{S}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{\lambda_2^a}\, \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \sqrt{\lambda_2^c}\, \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The vector $\bm{q}_{N,M}$ applies the budget constraint to the asset weights only, so that
$\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1$, leaving the currency exposures as a
zero-investment overlay. The matrix $\bm{L}^a$ applies the leverage constraint
to the asset weights only, giving $\|\bm{L}^a\bm{\theta}_t\|_1 = \|\bm{x}_t\|_1$. The diagonal regularization
matrices $\bm{S}_1$ and $\bm{S}_2$ allow the asset and currency penalties to differ within a
single objective
\begin{equation*}
    \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 = \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1,
    \qquad
    \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 = \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2.
\end{equation*}

Let $\bm{R}^{\text{all}} = [\, \bm{R}^{fh}\ \ \bm{R}^c \,] \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+M)}$ denote the
matrix of jointly sampled asset and currency returns, whose $k$-th row
$(\bm{R}^{\text{all}}_k)^\top = [(\bm{R}^{fh}_k)^\top, (\bm{e}_k - \bm{f}_k)^\top]$ is the $k$-th historical
realization of $\bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1}$. We replace the expectation in $F_\beta$ and the $L^2$ norm in $G_\beta$
by their empirical counterparts as in \Cref{app:linear-program-representation}, with the joint return
$\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1}$ in place of $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1}$.  Introducing $\alpha$ and
the auxiliary variables $u_k \ge 0$ for $k = 1, \dots, q$ as in \Cref{app:linear-program-representation}, the slack constraint \eqref{eq:slack_constraint} becomes
\begin{equation*}
    u_k \ge -\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_k - \alpha
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_k + \alpha + u_k \ge 0.
\end{equation*}
To linearize the $L^1$ penalty, we decompose all $N+M$ components of $\bm{\theta}_t$ into positive and negative parts
as in \Cref{app:l1}, $\bm{\theta}_t = \bm{\Delta}_t^+ - \bm{\Delta}_t^-$ with
$\bm{\Delta}_t^\pm = [(\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^\pm)^\top, (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^\pm)^\top]^\top$, so that at the optimum
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
    \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 &= \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+ + \bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-)
    + \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+ + \bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-), \\
    \|\bm{L}^a \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 &= \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-) \le \ell^a.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
The $L^2$ penalty is the quadratic form $\|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 = \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{S}_2^2\, \bm{\theta}_t$,
where $\bm{S}_2^2$ is diagonal with the blocks $\lambda_2^a \bm{I}_N$ and $\lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M$, which enter the
penalty matrix $\tilde{\bm{Q}}$ of the two programs below.

\subsection{Joint Optimization under Expected Shortfall} \label{app:joint_es}

For Expected Shortfall, the decision vector is
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \alpha,& \bm{u}^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+3M+q+1},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top,& 1,&
    \tfrac{1}{q(1-\beta)} \bm{1}_q^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^a \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} & \bm{0}_{N\times(2N + 2M + q + 1)} \\
        \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M\times(2N + 2M + q + 1)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q+1) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q+1) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q+1) \times (2N+2M+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The joint portfolio optimization problem under Expected Shortfall then becomes a quadratic program with linear
constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{0}_{q+1}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top \\
    \bm{I}_{N+M} & \bm{0}_{(N+M) \times (q+1)} & \bm{I}_{N+M} & -\bm{I}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^{\text{all}} & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times (N+M)} & \bm{0}_{q \times (N+M)} \\
    \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & 0 & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    & \quad u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q, \\[2pt]
    & \quad \Delta_{x,i,t}^\pm \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^\pm \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ c = 1, \dots, M.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In $\tilde{\bm{D}}$, the first row enforces the asset budget constraint $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1$,
and the remaining $N+M$ rows enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} for
all components of $\bm{\theta}_t$. In $\tilde{\bm{A}}$, the first $q$ rows encode the linearization
inequalities \eqref{eq:slack_constraint} applied to the joint return matrix $\bm{R}^{\text{all}}$.
The final row imposes the asset-only leverage constraint
\eqref{eq:leverage_constraint}, giving $\bm{1}_N^\top(\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+ + \bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-) = \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a$.

\subsection{Joint Optimization under Second-Order Expected Shortfall} \label{app:joint_soes}

For second-order Expected Shortfall, we introduce, as in \Cref{app:socp_soes}, one more auxiliary variable $s$
subject to the cone constraint $\|\bm{u}\|_2 \le s$, which extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \alpha,& \bm{u}^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top,& s \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+3M+q+2},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top,& 1,& \bm{0}_q^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,& \tfrac{1}{(1-\beta)\sqrt{q}} \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^a \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} & \bm{0}_{N\times(2N + 2M + q + 2)} \\
        \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M\times(2N + 2M + q + 2)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q+2) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q+2) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q+2) \times (2N+2M+q+2)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The joint portfolio optimization problem under second-order Expected Shortfall then becomes a second-order cone
program with a convex quadratic objective
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_socp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{0}_{q+1}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & 0 \\
    \bm{I}_{N+M} & \bm{0}_{(N+M) \times (q+1)} & \bm{I}_{N+M} & -\bm{I}_{N+M} & \bm{0}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\bm{R}^{\text{all}} & -\bm{1}_q & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times (N+M)} & \bm{0}_{q \times (N+M)} & \bm{0}_q \\
    \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & 0 & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & 0 \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \|\bm{u}\|_2 &\le s, \\[2pt]
    & \quad u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q, \\[2pt]
    & \quad \Delta_{x,i,t}^\pm \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^\pm \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ c = 1, \dots, M.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ and $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ are the constraint matrices of \eqref{eq:joint_qp} with a zero column
added for the variable $s$.

\section{Mean-Risk Optimization} \label{app:mean-risk}

The programs of Sections~\ref{app:linear-program-representation}--\ref{app:joint-optimization} minimize the risk
measure only. A mean-risk program subtracts the expected portfolio return, scaled by $1/\gamma$, from the risk measure, where
$\gamma > 0$ is the risk aversion coefficient. For problem \eqref{eq:risk_min}, this gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mean_es}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \rho_\beta\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, \bm{\mu}_t^\top \bm{x}_t \right\}, \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where $\bm{\mu}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}_{t+1}]$ is the expected asset return.

Let $\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and
$\bm{\mu}^c_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^M$ denote the expected fully hedged asset returns and
the expected excess currency returns, and $\bm{\mu}^{\text{all}}_t = [(\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)^\top, (\bm{\mu}^c_t)^\top]^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$. The asset means are estimated from the equally weighted return of each
economy, whose mean is shrunk toward zero with a James--Stein estimator,
and the currency means are set to the negative forward premium $-\bm{f}_t$. Both estimators are defined and further discussed in
\Cref{chap:optimization-framework}.

In the sequential approach, the asset step and the currency step become
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mean_sep_asset}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \rho_\beta\biggl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} \biggr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, (\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)^\top \bm{x}_t
    + \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,\ \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mean_sep_curr}
\begin{gathered}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm{\psi}_t^* \mid \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\psi}_t} \biggl\{ &
    \rho_\beta\biggl( \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t) \biggr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, (\bm{\mu}^c_t)^\top \bm{\psi}_t \\
    & + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2 \biggr\}
    \end{aligned} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 \le \ell^c.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the joint
approach, problem \eqref{eq:joint_obj} becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mean_joint}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{\theta}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\theta}_t} \left\{ \rho_\beta\biggl( \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} \biggr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, (\bm{\mu}^{\text{all}}_t)^\top \bm{\theta}_t
    + \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 + \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = 1, \quad \|\bm{L}^a \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a, \quad
    \|\bm{L}^c \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell^c,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{L}^c = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_{N \times N} & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}
applies the leverage constraint to the currency exposures only. We add the currency leverage constraint to both the
currency step and the joint program, since without it, the mean term can push the currency exposures
toward very large positions.
