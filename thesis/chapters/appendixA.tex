\chapter{Optimization Derivation: Expected Shortfall} \label{app:es_derivation}

In this appendix, we derive the optimization problems used in the main text,
building up from the Expected Shortfall objective to the implementable
quadratic programs solved in the empirical analysis.
In \Cref{app:es_auxiliary_formulation} we follow \cite{RockafellarUryasev2000} to derive the equivalence between direct
minimization of Expected Shortfall and an auxiliary formulation suitable for linear programming.
Following \cite{RegularizedMultiCurrencyExpectedShortfallPortfolios}, in \Cref{app:linear-program-representation} 
we embed this equivalence in a mean-ES objective,
by replacing the conditional expectation with its empirical counterpart, and linearizing the
terms to assemble the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem as a linear
program.
In \Cref{app:regularization} we introduce three regularization techniques, an $L_1$
penalty, an $L_2$ penalty, and a leverage constraint, giving us the 
base regularized quadratic program for portfolio optimization.
In \Cref{app:separate-optimization} we extend the framework to the multi-currency setting
under the separate (currency overlay) approach, deriving the asset and the currency quadratic programs.
Finally, in \Cref{app:joint-optimization} we derive the joint quadratic program formulation, 
in which asset and currency weights are optimized simultaneously
through a single Expected Shortfall term that captures the combined return distribution.

\section{Derivation of the Auxiliary Expected Shortfall Formulation} \label{app:es_auxiliary_formulation}

We derive the equivalence between the direct minimization of Expected Shortfall and the auxiliary formulation proposed by Rockafellar and Uryasev \cite{RockafellarUryasev2000} that allows for formulating the portfolio optimization as a linear program.

We assume that the loss distribution is continuous, meaning the loss cumulative distribution function has no jumps and the probability density function $f(l)$ exists. Under these assumptions, Value-at-Risk (VaR) is uniquely defined \cite{QuantitativeRiskManagement2016}.

Define the loss random variable at time $t+1$, given portfolio weights $x_t$, as $L(x_t) = -x_t^T R_{t+1}$. Let $f(l)$ denote the probability density function of the loss $L(x_t)$.\ The VaR at confidence level $\beta$ is defined as the $\beta$-quantile of the loss distribution
\begin{equation*}
    \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) = \min \{ \alpha : \mathbb{P}(L(x_t) \le \alpha) \ge \beta \}.
\end{equation*}

The Expected Shortfall at confidence level $\beta$ is defined as the conditional expectation of losses exceeding $\text{VaR}_\beta$
\begin{equation*}
    \text{ES}_\beta(x_t) = \mathbb{E}[L(x_t) \mid L(x_t) \ge \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)] = \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)}^\infty l f(l) \, dl.
\end{equation*}

We now consider the auxiliary function $F_\beta$ defined as
\begin{equation*}
    F_\beta(x_t, \alpha) = \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{E}_t[(L(x_t) - \alpha)^+].
\end{equation*}


We can rewrite the expectation term using the integral form
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}_t[(L(x_t) - \alpha)^+] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty (l - \alpha)^+ f(l) \, dl = \int_{\alpha}^\infty (l - \alpha) f(l) \, dl.
\end{equation*}

Before minimizing $F_\beta(x_t, \alpha)$ with respect to $\alpha$, we verify convexity. The second derivative of $F_\beta$ with respect to $\alpha$ is
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial F_\beta}{\partial \alpha} &= 1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\alpha}^\infty f(l) \, dl \\
    \frac{\partial^2 F_\beta}{\partial \alpha^2} &= \frac{1}{1-\beta} f(\alpha).
\end{align*}
Since the probability density function $f(\alpha) \ge 0$ and $\beta < 1$, the second derivative is non-negative, $\frac{\partial^2 F_\beta}{\partial \alpha^2} \ge 0$. Thus, $F_\beta(x_t, \alpha)$ is convex in $\alpha$, ensuring that the first-order condition is necessary and sufficient for a global minimum.

Differentiation of $F_\beta(x_t, \alpha)$ with respect to $\alpha$ yields the first-order condition for optimality. Using Leibniz's rule we have
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial F_\beta}{\partial \alpha} 
    &= 1 + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \frac{\partial}{\partial \alpha} \int_\alpha^\infty (l - \alpha) f(l) \, dl \\
    &= 1 + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \left[ -f(\alpha) \cdot 0 + \int_\alpha^\infty (-1) f(l) \, dl \right] \\
    &= 1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_\alpha^\infty f(l) \, dl \\
    &= 1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{P}(L(x_t) \ge \alpha).
\end{align*}

Setting the derivative to zero to find the optimal $\alpha^*$
\begin{align*}
    1 - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{P}(L(x_t) \ge \alpha^*) &= 0 \\
    \mathbb{P}(L(x_t) \ge \alpha^*) &= 1 - \beta \\
    \mathbb{P}(L(x_t) \le \alpha^*) &= \beta.
\end{align*}

This implies that the optimal $\alpha^*$ that minimizes the auxiliary function for a fixed $x_t$ is exactly the Value-at-Risk at level $\beta$, so $\alpha^* = \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)$.

Substituting $\alpha^* = \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)$ back into $F_\beta(x_t, \alpha)$ we obtain
\begin{align*}
    F_\beta(x_t, \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)) &= \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)}^\infty (l - \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)) f(l) \, dl \\
    &= \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \left( \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)}^\infty l f(l) \, dl - \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)}^\infty f(l) \, dl \right).
\end{align*}

Recognizing that $\int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)}^\infty f(l) \, dl = \mathbb{P}(L(x_t) \ge \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)) = 1-\beta$, we have
\begin{align*}
    F_\beta(x_t, \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)) &= \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \int_{\text{VaR}_\beta(x_t)}^\infty l f(l) \, dl - \frac{1}{1-\beta} \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) (1-\beta) \\
    &= \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) + \text{ES}_\beta(x_t) - \text{VaR}_\beta(x_t) \\
    &= \text{ES}_\beta(x_t).
\end{align*}

Thus, minimizing $\text{ES}_\beta(x_t)$ with respect to $x_t$ is equivalent to minimizing $F_\beta(x_t, \alpha)$ jointly with respect to $x_t$ and $\alpha$
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_opt}
    \min_{x_t} \text{ES}_\beta(x_t) = \min_{x_t, \alpha} \left( \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta} \mathbb{E}_t[(-x_t^T R_{t+1} - \alpha)^+] \right).
\end{equation}

Finally, we observe that $F_\beta(x_t, \alpha)$ is jointly convex in $(x_t, \alpha)$. The term $\alpha$ is linear (hence convex). The inner map $(x_t, \alpha) \mapsto (-x_t^\top R_{t+1} - \alpha)$ is affine. The outer function $z \mapsto z^+$ is convex and nondecreasing. The composition of a convex function with an affine map is convex \cite{boyd2004convex}. Thus $(-x_t^\top R_{t+1} - \alpha)^+$ is jointly convex in $(x_t, \alpha)$. Taking the expectation preserves convexity.


Since the objective function is convex, any local minimum is global, ensuring that optimization algorithms will converge efficiently to the optimal solution.

\section{Linear Program Representation of Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:linear-program-representation} % another possible title: Linear program representation of portfolio optimization

The equivalence in \eqref{eq:joint_opt} reduces Expected Shortfall minimization to the
jointly convex program
\begin{equation*}
    \min_{x_t} \text{ES}_\beta(x_t)
    = \min_{x_t, \alpha} \left( \alpha + \frac{1}{1-\beta}
    \mathbb{E}_t[(-x_t^\top R_{t+1} - \alpha)^+] \right).
\end{equation*}
This formulation is not yet appropriate for portfolio optimization, as it only optimizes for downside risk, without
taking into account the expected return of the portfolio. It also depends on the unknown
expectation $\mathbb{E}_t[\cdot]$. We address this by incorporating the expected return into the optimization
objective and replacing the expectation with its empirical counterpart, which yields a finite dimensional linear program.

Following \cite{RegularizedMultiCurrencyExpectedShortfallPortfolios} and \cite{goldberg2013minimizingshortfall}, we trade off expected return against %
downside risk through a relative risk-aversion coefficient $\gamma > 0$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mean_es}
    x_t^* = \arg\min_{x_t} \left\{ -\mu_t^\top x_t + \frac{\gamma}{2}
    \text{ES}_\beta(R_{P,t+1}) \right\}, \quad \text{s.t. } x_t^\top \mathbf{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t$ denotes the asset return mean estimator and the budget constraint
$x_t^\top \mathbf{1}_N = 1$ enforces full investment. Substituting the auxiliary
representation of $\text{ES}_\beta$ into \eqref{eq:mean_es} gives
\begin{equation*}
    x_t^*, \alpha^* = \arg\min_{x_t, \alpha} \left\{ -\mu_t^\top x_t
    + \frac{\gamma}{2} \alpha
    + \frac{\gamma}{2(1-\beta)} \mathbb{E}_t[(-x_t^\top R_{t+1} - \alpha)^+] \right\}.
\end{equation*}

Let $y_1, \dots, y_q \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote $q$ historical realizations of the joint
return vector $R_{t+1}$. We approximate the conditional expectation by the empirical
average over this sample,
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}_t[(-x_t^\top R_{t+1} - \alpha)^+]
    \approx \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q (-x_t^\top y_k - \alpha)^+,
\end{equation*}
so that the optimization problem becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:empirical_es}
    x_t^*, \alpha^* = \arg\min_{x_t, \alpha} \left\{ -\mu_t^\top x_t
    + \frac{\gamma}{2} \alpha
    + \frac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} \sum_{k=1}^q (-x_t^\top y_k - \alpha)^+ \right\},
    \quad \text{s.t. } x_t^\top \mathbf{1}_N = 1.
\end{equation}

The summand $(-x_t^\top y_k - \alpha)^+$ is convex but non-linear. We linearize it by
introducing, for each observation $k$, an auxiliary variable $u_k \ge 0$ subject to
\begin{equation*}
    u_k \ge -x_t^\top y_k - \alpha
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    x_t^\top y_k + \alpha + u_k \ge 0.
\end{equation*}
Since the objective in \eqref{eq:empirical_es} is strictly increasing in each $u_k$
(its coefficient $\tfrac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} > 0$) and minimized, every $u_k$ is driven
to its lower bound at the optimum, so $u_k^* = (-x_t^\top y_k - \alpha)^+$. The slack
constraints therefore reproduce the term $(-x_t^\top y_k - \alpha)^+$ while keeping all
relations linear.


By collecting the decision variables in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{x}_t = [x_{1,t}, \dots, x_{N,t}, \alpha, u_1, \dots, u_q]^\top
    = [x_t^\top, \alpha, u^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+q+1)},
\end{equation*}
and constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    c = \begin{bmatrix} -\mu_t^\top, &  \dfrac{\gamma}{2}, & \dfrac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} \mathbf{1}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+q+1)},
\end{equation*}
the optimization problem \eqref{eq:empirical_es} can be written as a linear program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:base_lp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \tilde{x}_t^* = \arg\min_{\tilde{x}_t} \quad
     c^\top \tilde{x}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & D \tilde{x}_t =   
    \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{1}_N^\top & 0_{q+1}^\top \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{x}_t = E = 1, \\[2pt]
    & A \tilde{x}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -R^{\text{data}} & -\mathbf{1}_q & -I_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{x}_t \le b = 0_q, \\[2pt]
    & \alpha \ge 0, \quad u_j \ge 0 \quad \text{for } j = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $D \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (N+q+1)}$ encodes the budget constraint, while
$A \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+q+1)}$ and $b \in \mathbb{R}^{q}$ collect the
$q$ inequality constraints arising from the Rockafellar and Uryasev (2000)
representation. The matrix $R^{\text{data}} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ stacks the
sampled return vectors $y_k^\top$ row by row, $0_q$ is the $q$-dimensional zero vector,
and $I_q$ the $q$-dimensional identity matrix.

Problem \eqref{eq:base_lp} has a linear objective subject to one linear equality and $q$
linear inequalities, and is thus a linear program solvable with standard methods. This
formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under Expected Shortfall. 
Regularization terms and additional constraints are added in the following section \ref{app:regularization} of the appendix. 
\section{Regularization} \label{app:regularization}

The base problem \eqref{eq:base_lp} is known to be numerically unstable in
high-dimensional settings [add citation], producing extreme and concentrated weights. We stabilize it
with three regularization techniques. An $L_1$-norm penalty that promotes sparsity, an $L_2$-norm penalty
that promotes shrinkage, and a leverage constraint that bounds gross exposure. Each is
appended to \eqref{eq:base_lp} while preserving its linear or quadratic structure. The
$L_1$ penalty and the leverage constraint share a common variable decomposition, which
we introduce first.

\subsection{$L_1$ Regularization} \label{app:l1}

The $L_1$ penalty $\lambda_1 \|x_t\|_1 = \lambda_1 \sum_{i=1}^N |x_{i,t}|$, with
$\lambda_1 \ge 0$, is non-linear through the absolute value. Following Roncalli (2013),
we linearize it by splitting each weight into its positive and negative parts via the
auxiliary variables $\Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0$ and $\Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:l1_decomp}
    x_{i,t} = \Delta_{i,t}^+ - \Delta_{i,t}^-,
    \qquad
    |x_{i,t}| = \Delta_{i,t}^+ + \Delta_{i,t}^-.
\end{equation}
The second identity holds at the optimum: minimizing the penalty drives the parts to
their smallest feasible values, so at most one of $\Delta_{i,t}^+$, $\Delta_{i,t}^-$ is
non-zero, giving $\Delta_{i,t}^+ = \max(x_{i,t}, 0)$ and
$\Delta_{i,t}^- = \max(-x_{i,t}, 0)$. The penalty is then linear in the auxiliary
variables,
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda_1 \|x_t\|_1 = \lambda_1 \mathbf{1}_N^\top (\Delta_t^+ + \Delta_t^-).
\end{equation*}

We extend the decision vector to carry the two parts,
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{x}_t = [\, x_t^\top,\ \alpha,\ u^\top,\ (\Delta_t^-)^\top,\ (\Delta_t^+)^\top \,]^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q+1},
\end{equation*}
and append the penalty coefficients to the cost vector,
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{c} = [\, -\mu_t^\top,\ \tfrac{\gamma}{2},\
    \tfrac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} \mathbf{1}_q^\top,\
    \lambda_1 \mathbf{1}_N^\top,\ \lambda_1 \mathbf{1}_N^\top \,]^\top.
\end{equation*}
The decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} is enforced as $N$ additional linear equality
constraints, $x_t + \Delta_t^- - \Delta_t^+ = 0_N$, alongside the original budget
constraint. All relations remain linear, so the optimization problem is still a linear program after the addition of the $L_1$ penalty.

\subsection{$L_2$ Regularization} \label{app:l2}

The $L_2$ penalty $\lambda_2 \|x_t\|_2^2 = \lambda_2 x_t^\top x_t$, with
$\lambda_2 \ge 0$, is quadratic in the weights and is written as a quadratic form,
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda_2 \|x_t\|_2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\, x_t^\top Q\, x_t,
    \qquad Q = 2 \lambda_2 I_N.
\end{equation*}
Embedding this in the augmented vector, the penalty becomes
$\tfrac{1}{2}\, \tilde{x}_t^\top \tilde{Q}\, \tilde{x}_t$ with $\tilde{Q}$ placing the
block $2\lambda_2 I_N$ on the asset weights and zeros on the auxiliary variables,
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{Q} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        2 \lambda_2 I_N & 0_{N \times (2N+q+1)} \\
        0_{(2N+q+1) \times N} & 0_{(2N+q+1) \times (2N+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Adding this term turns the $L_1-$augmented linear objective of the problem \eqref{eq:base_lp} into a quadratic objective
$\tilde{c}^\top \tilde{x}_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \tilde{x}_t^\top \tilde{Q}\, \tilde{x}_t$.
Since $\tilde{Q}$ is positive semi-definite, the objective remains convex, and the optimization problem becomes a
quadratic program with linear constraints.

\subsection{Leverage Constraint} \label{app:leverage}

The leverage constraint $\|x_t\|_1 \le \ell$, with $\ell \ge 1$, bounds the portfolio's
gross exposure and guarantees a bounded feasible region even when $\lambda_1 = 0$. Reusing
the decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp}, it is expressed as a single linear inequality
\begin{equation*}
    \|x_t\|_1 = \mathbf{1}_N^\top (\Delta_t^+ + \Delta_t^-) \le \ell.
\end{equation*}
The choice $\ell = 1$ rules out leverage entirely and is equivalent to a hard $L_1$
constraint. As a linear inequality, it preserves the quadratic program structure.

\subsection{Quadratic Program Representation of the Regularized Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:reg_qp}

Adding all three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:base_lp}, makes the portfolio optimization problem under Expected Shortfall 
a quadratic program with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:reg_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{x}_t^* &= \arg\min_{\tilde{x}_t} \quad
    \tilde{c}^\top \tilde{x}_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \tilde{x}_t^\top \tilde{Q}\, \tilde{x}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{D} \tilde{x}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{1}_N^\top & 0_{q+1}^\top & 0_N^\top & 0_N^\top \\
    I_N & 0_{N \times (q+1)} & I_N & -I_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{x}_t
    = \tilde{E} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{A} \tilde{x}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -R^{\text{data}} & -\mathbf{1}_q & -I_q & O_{q \times N} & O_{q \times N} \\
    0_N^\top & 0 & 0_q^\top & \mathbf{1}_N^\top & \mathbf{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{x}_t
    \le b = \begin{bmatrix} 0_q \\ \ell \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    & \alpha \ge 0, \quad u_j \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ j = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{D}$ enforces the budget constraint and the remaining $N$ rows
enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp}. The first $q$ rows of $\tilde{A}$
are the Rockafellar and Uryasev (2000) inequalities extended with zeros for the new
variables, and the final row imposes the leverage constraint.

\section{Separate (Overlay) Optimization} \label{app:separate-optimization}
In the separate (currency overlay) approach, the asset and currency allocations are
optimized in two sequential steps. First, the asset weights $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ are
chosen on the fully hedged returns, then, conditional on the optimal $x_t^*$, the
currency exposures $\psi_t \in \mathbb{R}^M$ are optimized.
Let 
\begin{align*}
\mu^{fh} &= \mathbb{E}[R^{fh}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N \\
\mu^c &= \mathbb{E}[e_{t+1} - f_t] \in \mathbb{R}^M
\end{align*}
denote the expected fully hedged asset return and the expected
excess currency return respectively. The two otimization steps are then given by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sep_asset_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    x_t^* = \arg\min_{x_t} \left\{ -x_t^\top \mu^{fh}
    + \tfrac{\gamma}{2} \text{ES}_\beta(x_t^\top R^{fh}_{t+1})
    + \lambda_1^a \|x_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^a \|x_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } x_t^\top \mathbf{1}_N = 1,\ \|x_t\|_1 \le \ell,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \psi_t^* \mid x_t^* = \arg\min_{\psi_t} \left\{ -\psi_t^\top \mu^c
    + \tfrac{\gamma}{2} \text{ES}_\beta\!\left( x_t^{*\top} R^{fh}_{t+1}
    + \psi_t^\top (e_{t+1} - f_t) \right)
    + \lambda_1^c \|\psi_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^c \|\psi_t\|_2^2 \right\}.
    \label{eq:sep_curr_obj}
\end{equation}
The regularization parameters are kept separate across assets ($\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$)
and currencies ($\lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c$). 

\subsection{Asset Optimization} \label{app:sep_asset}

The asset step \eqref{eq:sep_asset_obj} is identical to the regularized
single-currency program \eqref{eq:reg_qp}. It carries a budget constraint, an $L_1$
and $L_2$ penalty terms, and a leverage constraint.
It is therefore obtained directly from \eqref{eq:reg_qp} by substituting the matrix of historically sampled fully hedged
returns $R^{fh}\in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ for $R^{\text{data}}$, the mean $\mu^{fh}$ for $\mu_t$, and the
asset-specific penalties $\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$. With the augmented vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{x}_t = [\, x_t^\top,\ \alpha_x,\ u_x^\top,\
    (\Delta_{x,t}^-)^\top,\ (\Delta_{x,t}^+)^\top \,]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q+1},
\end{equation*}
the cost vector and penalty matrix are
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{c} = [\, -(\mu^{fh})^\top,\ \tfrac{\gamma}{2},\
    \tfrac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} \mathbf{1}_q^\top,\
    \lambda_1^a \mathbf{1}_N^\top,\ \lambda_1^a \mathbf{1}_N^\top \,]^\top,
    \qquad
    \tilde{Q} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        2 \lambda_2^a I_N & 0_{N \times (2N+q+1)} \\
        0_{(2N+q+1) \times N} & 0_{(2N+q+1) \times (2N+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
and the equality, inequality, and sign constraints are exactly those of
\eqref{eq:reg_qp} with $R^{fh}$ in place of $R^{\text{data}}$. The step yields the optimal
asset weights $x_t^*$, which are then fixed in the currency overlay optimization.

\subsection{Currency Overlay Optimization} \label{app:sep_currency}

Conditional on $x_t^*$, the currency step \eqref{eq:sep_curr_obj} minimizes the Expected
Shortfall of the combined return $x_t^{*\top} R^{fh}_{t+1} + \psi_t^\top (e_{t+1} - f_t)$
over the currency exposures $\psi_t$. Applying the Rockafellar and Uryasev (2000)
representation and the empirical approximation as in
Appendix~\ref{app:linear-program-representation}, we introduce the auxiliary variable
$\alpha_\psi$ and slacks $u_{k,\psi} \ge 0$. Since the asset leg is now fixed, the
slack constraint becomes
\begin{equation*}
    u_{k,\psi} \ge -\psi_t^\top (e_k - f_t) - x_t^{*\top} R^{fh}_k - \alpha_\psi
    \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
    -\psi_t^\top (e_k - f_t) - \alpha_\psi - u_{k,\psi} \le x_t^{*\top} R^{fh}_k,
\end{equation*}
where $R^{fh}_k$ is the $k$-th sampled fully hedged return vector. The fixed asset-portfolio
returns therefore appear on the right-hand side. Collecting them in the vector
$R^{fh}_P = R^{fh} x_t^* \in \mathbb{R}^q$ and linearizing the currency $L_1$ penalty with
the decomposition $\psi_{c,t} = \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ - \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^-$ as in
Appendix~\ref{app:l1}, we form the augmented vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\psi}_t = [\, \psi_t^\top,\ \alpha_\psi,\ u_\psi^\top,\
    (\Delta_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,\ (\Delta_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \,]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3M+q+1}.
\end{equation*}
The currency overlay is then the quadratic program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:sep_curr_qp}
\begin{gathered}
    \psi_t^* = \arg\min_{\tilde{\psi}_t} \quad
    \tilde{c}^\top \tilde{\psi}_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \tilde{\psi}_t^\top \tilde{Q}\, \tilde{\psi}_t, \\[2pt]
    \tilde{c} = [\, -(\mu^c)^\top,\ \tfrac{\gamma}{2},\
    \tfrac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} \mathbf{1}_q^\top,\
    \lambda_1^c \mathbf{1}_M^\top,\ \lambda_1^c \mathbf{1}_M^\top \,]^\top, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{Q} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        2 \lambda_2^c I_M & 0_{M \times (2M+q+1)} \\
        0_{(2M+q+1) \times M} & 0_{(2M+q+1) \times (2M+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{D} \tilde{\psi}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} I_M & 0_{M \times (q+1)} & I_M & -I_M \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\psi}_t
    = \tilde{E} = 0_M, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{A} \tilde{\psi}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -R^c & -\mathbf{1}_q & -I_q & O_{q \times M} & O_{q \times M} \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\psi}_t \le b = R^{fh}_P = R^{fh} x_t^*, \\[4pt]
    \alpha_\psi \ge 0, \quad u_{j,\psi} \ge 0, \quad
    \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^- \ge 0, \quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } c = 1, \dots, M,\ j = 1, \dots, q.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

The currency optimization differs from the asset optimization \eqref{eq:reg_qp} in the following ways.
First, there is no budget constraint, the currency portfolio is a
zero-investment portfolio, so the domestic exposure $\psi_{0,t}$ is inferred residually and
the equality block $\tilde{D}$ enforces only the weight decomposition, with right-hand
side $\tilde{E} = 0_M$ rather than $[1, 0_M^\top]^\top$. Second, the fixed asset leg
shifts the inequality right-hand side from $0_q$ to $R^{fh}_P = R^{fh} x_t^*$. Third, no
leverage constraint is imposed on the currency exposures, so $\tilde{A}$ contains only the
$q$ Rockafellar and Uryasev (2000) rows. The $L_1$ and $L_2$ regularizing terms are
as before, through the last two blocks of $\tilde{c}$ and the main block of
$\tilde{Q}$, so the currency overlay remains a convex quadratic program.

\section{Joint Optimization} \label{app:joint-optimization}

In the joint approach, the asset and currency decisions are made simultaneously rather
than sequentially. We stack them into the combined decision vector
$\theta_t = (x_t, \psi_t)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$ and the combined return vector
$R_{t+1} = (R^{fh}_{t+1}, e_{t+1} - f_t)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$, with joint mean
$\mu = \mathbb{E}[R_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$. The joint problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \theta_t^* = \arg\min_{\theta_t} \left\{ -\theta_t^\top \mu
    + \tfrac{\gamma}{2} \text{ES}_\beta(\theta_t^\top R_{t+1})
    + \|S_1 \theta_t\|_1 + \|S_2 \theta_t\|_2^2 \right\}, \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \theta_t^\top q_{N,M} = 1, \quad \|L \theta_t\|_1 \le \ell.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The combined return satisfies
$\theta_t^\top R_{t+1} = x_t^\top R^{fh}_{t+1} + \psi_t^\top (e_{t+1} - f_t)$, so a single
Expected Shortfall term now depends jointly on all weights, removing the two-step
structure of the separate approach.

Four structured objects adapt the single-currency machinery to the joint setting, all
respecting the asymmetry between assets and currencies:
\begin{equation*}
    q_{N,M} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{1}_N \\ 0_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    L = \begin{bmatrix} I_N & 0_{N \times M} \\ 0_{M \times N} & 0_{M \times M} \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    S_1 = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^a I_N & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_1^c I_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    S_2 = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{\lambda_2^a}\, I_N & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{\lambda_2^c}\, I_M \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The selector $q_{N,M}$ applies the budget constraint to the asset block only, leaving the
currency exposures as a zero-investment hedge; the diagonal matrix $L$ likewise applies
the leverage constraint to the assets only, so that $\|L\theta_t\|_1 = \|x_t\|_1$. The
diagonal regularization matrices $S_1$ and $S_2$ let the asset and currency penalties
differ within one objective,
\begin{equation*}
    \|S_1 \theta_t\|_1 = \lambda_1^a \|x_t\|_1 + \lambda_1^c \|\psi_t\|_1,
    \qquad
    \|S_2 \theta_t\|_2^2 = \lambda_2^a \|x_t\|_2^2 + \lambda_2^c \|\psi_t\|_2^2,
\end{equation*}
where the square roots in $S_2$ ensure the squared norm reproduces the intended
coefficients $\lambda_2^a, \lambda_2^c$.

We apply the Rockafellar and Uryasev (2000) representation and the empirical approximation
of Appendix~\ref{app:linear-program-representation} once to the joint return
$\theta_t^\top R_{t+1}$, introducing the auxiliary variable $\alpha$ and slacks
$u_k \ge 0$, and linearize the $L_1$ penalty by decomposing all $N+M$ components of
$\theta_t$ into positive and negative parts as in Appendix~\ref{app:l1}. Collecting the
asset and currency parts separately, we form the augmented vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\theta}_t = [\, x_t^\top,\ \psi_t^\top,\ \alpha,\ u^\top,\
    (\Delta_{x,t}^-)^\top,\ (\Delta_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,\
    (\Delta_{x,t}^+)^\top,\ (\Delta_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \,]^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+3M+q+1}.
\end{equation*}
With $R^{\text{all}} = [\, R^{fh} \ \ R^c \,] \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+M)}$ the matrix of
jointly sampled asset and currency returns, the joint problem is the quadratic program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:joint_qp}
\begin{gathered}
    \tilde{\theta}_t^* = \arg\min_{\tilde{\theta}_t} \quad
    \tilde{c}^\top \tilde{\theta}_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \tilde{\theta}_t^\top \tilde{Q}\, \tilde{\theta}_t, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{c} = [\, -\mu^\top,\ \tfrac{\gamma}{2},\
    \tfrac{\gamma}{2q(1-\beta)} \mathbf{1}_q^\top,\
    \lambda_1^a \mathbf{1}_N^\top,\ \lambda_1^c \mathbf{1}_M^\top,\
    \lambda_1^a \mathbf{1}_N^\top,\ \lambda_1^c \mathbf{1}_M^\top \,]^\top, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{Q} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        2 \lambda_2^a I_N & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 2 \lambda_2^c I_M & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0_{(2N+2M+q+1) \times (2N+2M+q+1)}
    \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{D} \tilde{\theta}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} q_{N,M}^\top & 0_{q+1}^\top & 0_{N+M}^\top & 0_{N+M}^\top \\
    I_{N+M} & 0_{(N+M) \times (q+1)} & I_{N+M} & -I_{N+M} \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\theta}_t
    = \tilde{E} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0_{N+M} \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \tilde{A} \tilde{\theta}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -R^{\text{all}} & -\mathbf{1}_q & -I_q & O_{q \times (N+M)} & O_{q \times (N+M)} \\
    0_{N+M}^\top & 0_{q+1}^\top & q_{N,M}^\top & q_{N,M}^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\theta}_t
    \le b = \begin{bmatrix} 0_q \\ \ell \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \alpha \ge 0, \quad u_j \ge 0, \quad
    \Delta_{x,i,t}^\pm \ge 0, \quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^\pm \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ c = 1, \dots, M,\ j = 1, \dots, q.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{D}$ imposes the asset budget through the selector
$q_{N,M}^\top = [\mathbf{1}_N^\top,\ 0_M^\top]$, and the remaining $N+M$ rows enforce the
weight decomposition for the combined vector. In $\tilde{A}$, the first $q$ rows are the
Rockafellar and Uryasev (2000) inequalities on the joint return matrix
$R^{\text{all}}$, with right-hand side $0_q$ — in contrast to the conditional currency
step \eqref{eq:sep_curr_qp}, no asset leg is pre-fixed, so nothing shifts to the
right-hand side. The final row imposes the asset-only leverage constraint, where
$q_{N,M}^\top$ again selects the asset parts of the decomposition, giving
$\mathbf{1}_N^\top(\Delta_{x,t}^+ + \Delta_{x,t}^-) = \|x_t\|_1 \le \ell$.

The joint formulation thus solves a single quadratic program over the combined vector,
capturing the dependencies between asset and currency exposures directly through the
joint Expected Shortfall term and the off-diagonal return covariation embedded in
$R^{\text{all}}$. This comes at the cost of a higher-dimensional problem
($3N + 3M + q + 1$ variables) relative to the two smaller programs of the separate
approach, which is the trade-off examined empirically in the main text.
