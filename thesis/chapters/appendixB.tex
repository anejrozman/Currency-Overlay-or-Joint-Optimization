\chapter{Optimization Derivation: Mean-Variance} \label{app:mv_derivation}

In this appendix, we derive the optimization problems used in the main text for mean-variance portfolio 
optimization. We start with the standard mean-variance formulation and then introduce regularization techniques, 
separate optimization for assets and currency overlay, and finally the joint optimization problem.
In \Cref{app:mv_qp_representation}, we follow \citep{SparseAndStableInternationalPortfolioOptimization, Markowitz1952} and 
present the quadratic program representation of the mean-variance portfolio optimization problem.





\section{Quadratic Program Representation of the Mean-Variance Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:mv_qp_representation}

The mean-variance portfolio optimization of \cite{Markowitz1952} trades off the expected
portfolio return against its return variance. The portfolio return
$\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1}$ is linear in the asset weights, so its variance is the
quadratic form $\mathrm{Var}(\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1}) = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}_t \bm{x}_t$,
where $\bm{R}_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the asset return vector and $\bm{\Sigma}_t$
its covariance matrix. Following the international mean-variance formulation of
\cite{SparseAndStableInternationalPortfolioOptimization}, we write the problem as a minimization and trade off
expected return against variance through a relative risk-aversion coefficient
$\gamma > 0$
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_objective}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ -\bm{\mu}_t^\top \bm{x}_t
    + \frac{\gamma}{2} \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}_t \bm{x}_t \right\},
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where $\bm{\mu}_t$ denotes the asset return mean estimator, $\bm{\Sigma}_t$ the asset
return covariance estimator, and the budget constraint $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1$
enforces full investment.

In contrast to the Expected Shortfall objective \eqref{eq:mean_es}, which depends on
the unknown conditional expectation $\mathbb{E}_t[\cdot]$ and requires the auxiliary
representation of \Cref{app:es_auxiliary_formulation} together with a sample
approximation to obtain a tractable form, the variance term in \eqref{eq:mv_objective}
is already expressed in closed quadratic form. Problem \eqref{eq:mv_objective} is
therefore already formulated as a quadratic program.

The objective in \eqref{eq:mv_objective} is convex. The first term
$-\bm{\mu}_t^\top \bm{x}_t$ is linear, hence convex, and the quadratic term
$\tfrac{\gamma}{2} \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}_t \bm{x}_t$ has Hessian
$\gamma \bm{\Sigma}_t$. Since a covariance matrix is positive semi-definite and
$\gamma > 0$, this Hessian is positive semi-definite, so the quadratic term, and with
it the full objective, is convex \cite{boyd2004convex}. Any local minimum is therefore
global, ensuring the problem can be solved reliably with standard convex optimization
methods.

The population mean and covariance are unknown and replaced by their time-$t$ sample
estimators $\bm{\mu}_t$ and $\bm{\Sigma}_t$. Because the variance enters in closed form
through $\bm{\Sigma}_t$, this plug-in step introduces no auxiliary variables and leaves
the decision vector at dimension $N$, independent of the number of historical
observations $q$. This is different from the empirical approximation of the Expected
Shortfall objective in \Cref{app:linear-program-representation}, where the conditional
expectation is replaced by an average over $q$ historical return samples and $q$ slack
variables $u_k$ enlarge the problem to dimension $N + q + 1$. The sample covariance
estimator is positive semi-definite, so the convexity established above is preserved
under estimation. The choice of estimators is discussed in \Cref{app:mv_bayes_stein}.

Collecting the asset weights in the decision vector $\bm{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and
defining the cost vector and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{c} = -\bm{\mu}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N, \qquad
    \bm{Q} = \gamma \bm{\Sigma}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N},
\end{equation*}
the mean-variance problem \eqref{eq:mv_objective} is written as the quadratic program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_base_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \bm{x}_t^* &= \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \quad
    \bm{c}^\top \bm{x}_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{Q}\, \bm{x}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad \bm{D} \bm{x}_t &= \bm{1}_N^\top \bm{x}_t = E = 1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} = \bm{1}_N^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$ encodes the budget constraint
with right-hand side $E = 1$.

Problem \eqref{eq:mv_base_qp} has a convex quadratic objective subject to a single
linear equality constraint, and is thus a quadratic program solvable with standard
methods.
This formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under
the mean-variance criterion. Regularization terms and additional constraints are added
in \Cref{app:mv_regularization}.
