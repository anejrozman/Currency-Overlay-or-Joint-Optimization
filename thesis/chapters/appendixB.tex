\chapter{Optimization Derivation: Variance and Semi-Variance} \label{app:mv_derivation}

In this appendix, we derive the optimization problems used in the main text for mean-variance portfolio 
optimization. We start with the standard mean-variance formulation and then introduce regularization techniques and 
estimators to stabilize the optimization. We then extend the formulation to the multi-currency setting, considering
separate optimization for assets and currency overlay, and finally the joint optimization problem.
In \Cref{app:mv_qp_representation}, we follow \citep{SparseAndStableInternationalPortfolioOptimization, Markowitz1952} and 
present the quadratic program representation of the mean-variance portfolio optimization problem.





\section{Definitions and Convexity} \label{app:mv_definitions}

We define the variance of the portfolio return, which Markowitz \cite{Markowitz1952} uses as the measure of portfolio
risk, and the semi-variance, which Markowitz et al.~\cite{Markowitz1993} consider as a downside alternative to the
variance, and show that both are convex in the portfolio weights. The two definitions allow for writing the portfolio
optimization problem as a quadratic program for both risk measures in \Cref{app:mv_representation}.

Let $\bm{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote the portfolio weights, $\bm{R}_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the asset return vector
and $\bm{\mu}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the expected asset return. The variance of the
portfolio return is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:variance_def}
    \text{Var}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    = \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ (\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\mu}_t - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1})^2 \bigr]
    = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}_t \bm{x}_t,
\end{equation}
where $\bm{\Sigma}_t = \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ (\bm{R}_{t+1} - \bm{\mu}_t)(\bm{R}_{t+1} - \bm{\mu}_t)^\top \bigr]
\in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the conditional covariance matrix of the asset returns. Since the variance \eqref{eq:variance_def} is non-negative for every $\bm{x}_t$, the matrix
$\bm{\Sigma}_t$ is positive semi-definite, and the quadratic form is convex in $\bm{x}_t$
\cite[Section~3.1.4]{boyd2004convex}. 

The variance penalizes returns above the expected return as much as returns below it, although an investor is typically
concerned with underperformance rather than outperformance \cite{Markowitz1993}. This motivates us to consider the semi-variance of the portfolio return
as a downside risk measure. The semi-variance is defined as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:svar_def}
    \text{SVar}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    = \mathbb{E}_t\Bigl[ \bigl( (\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\mu}_t - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1})^+ \bigr)^2 \Bigr].
\end{equation}

The inner map $\bm{x}_t \mapsto \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{\mu}_t - \bm{R}_{t+1})$ is linear and the function
$z \mapsto (z^+)^2$ is convex. The composition of a convex function with
an affine map is convex \cite[Section~3.2.2]{boyd2004convex}. Expectation is a
linear operator which preserves convexity, so the semi-variance is convex in $\bm{x}_t$.

Since both the variance and the semi-variance are convex, any local minimum is global, ensuring that the portfolio
optimization problems under both risk measures can be solved reliably with standard convex optimization methods.



\section{Representation for Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:mv_representation}

Let $\rho$ denote either of the two risk measures, written as a function of the portfolio return
$\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1}$. The portfolio optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_risk_min}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \rho\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr), \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where the budget constraint enforces full investment. Each program in this appendix defines its own decision vector,
vector of constants and penalty and constraint matrices, so the same symbols denote slightly different objects in
different programs, and we refer to a specific program by its equation number when discussing them.

\subsection{Variance} \label{app:mv_var_qp} 

By \eqref{eq:variance_def}, the objective of problem \eqref{eq:mv_risk_min} under variance is the quadratic form
$\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}_t \bm{x}_t$. In the programs, $\bm{\Sigma}_t$ is the Ledoit--Wolf estimator of the covariance
matrix defined in \Cref{chap:optimization-framework}, which is positive semi-definite, so the convexity shown in
\Cref{app:mv_definitions} is preserved. With the decision vector $\bm{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and the penalty matrix
$\bm{Q} = \bm{\Sigma}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$, the portfolio optimization problem under
variance can be written as a quadratic program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:var_base_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \quad
     \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{Q}\, \bm{x}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \bm{D} \bm{x}_t = \bm{1}_N^\top \bm{x}_t = E = 1.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} = \bm{1}_N^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times N}$ encodes the budget constraint. This formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under variance.

\subsection{Semi-Variance} \label{app:mv_svar_qp}

Let $\bm{y}_1, \dots, \bm{y}_q \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote $q$ historical realizations of the asset return vector
$\bm{R}_{t+1}$, as in \Cref{app:lp_es}. We approximate the expected asset return $\bm{\mu}_t$ by the sample mean
$\bar{\bm{y}} = \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \bm{y}_k$ and the conditional expectation in \eqref{eq:svar_def} by the
empirical average over this sample
\begin{equation*}
    \text{SVar}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    \approx \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \bigl( (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+ \bigr)^2,
\end{equation*}
so that the portfolio optimization problem under semi-variance becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:empirical_svar}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \bigl( (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+ \bigr)^2,
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1.
\end{equation}
Each summand is only piecewise quadratic in $\bm{x}_t$. Following \cite{Markowitz1993}, and as for Expected Shortfall in \Cref{app:lp_es},
we modify it by introducing, for each observation $k$, an auxiliary variable
$u_k \ge 0$ subject to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:svar_slack}
    u_k \ge \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k 
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}}) + u_k \ge 0.
\end{equation}
The objective becomes $\frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q u_k^2 = \frac{1}{q}
\|\bm{u}\|_2^2$. It is strictly increasing in each $u_k \ge 0$ and minimized, so every $u_k$ is driven to its lower
bound at the optimum, $u_k^* = (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+$.

By collecting the decision variables in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} x_{1,t}, \dots, x_{N,t}, & u_1, \dots, u_q \end{bmatrix}^\top
    = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top, & \bm{u}^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q},
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{Q} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_{N \times N} & \bm{0}_{N \times q} \\ \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \tfrac{1}{q} \bm{I}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+q) \times (N+q)},
\end{equation*}
the portfolio optimization problem \eqref{eq:empirical_svar} can be written as a quadratic program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:svar_base_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* = \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \bm{Q}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \bm{D} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = E = 1, \\[2pt]
    & \bm{A} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{I}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \le \bm{b} = \bm{0}_q, \\[2pt]
    & u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (N+q)}$ encodes the budget constraint, while $\bm{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+q)}$
and $\bm{b} \in \mathbb{R}^q$ encode the $q$ inequality constraints \eqref{eq:svar_slack}. The matrix
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} = \bm{R}^{\text{data}} - \bm{1}_q \bar{\bm{y}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ is the
demeaned sample matrix, where $\bm{R}^{\text{data}}$ is comprised of the sampled return vectors $\bm{y}_k^\top$ row by
row as in \Cref{app:lp_es}, so that the $k$-th row of $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}}$ is $(\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}})^\top$.
This formulation serves as the base
unregularized portfolio optimization problem under semi-variance.


\section{Regularization} \label{app:mv_regularization}

The base problems \eqref{eq:var_base_qp} and \eqref{eq:svar_base_qp} are known to be unstable in high-dimensional
settings, where the programs are prone to producing extreme and concentrated weights \citep{Michaud1989, kondor2007noise}. As for
Expected Shortfall, we stabilize them with the $L^1$ penalty, the $L^2$ penalty and the asset leverage constraint of
Sections~\ref{app:l1}--\ref{app:leverage}, using the decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} of the weights into positive
and negative parts and the leverage constraint \eqref{eq:leverage_constraint}.

\subsection{Variance} \label{app:mv_var_reg}

Adding the three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:var_base_qp} extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \bm{\Sigma}_t + \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times 2N} \\
        \bm{0}_{2N \times N} & \bm{0}_{2N \times 2N}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}_t \bm{x}_t + \lambda_2 \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{x}_t
= \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{\Sigma}_t + \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N) \bm{x}_t$, the $L^2$ penalty shrinks the covariance estimate toward
a scaled identity matrix \cite{Li2015}. The portfolio optimization problem under variance then becomes a quadratic program
with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:var_reg_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t + \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top \\
    \bm{I}_N & \bm{I}_N & -\bm{I}_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    \le \tilde{b} = \ell^a, \\[6pt]
     \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforces the budget constraint and the remaining $N$ rows enforce the weight
decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp}, while the single row of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ imposes the leverage constraint
\eqref{eq:leverage_constraint}.

\subsection{Semi-Variance} \label{app:mv_svar_reg}

Adding the same three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:svar_base_qp} extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{u}^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top,& \bm{0}_q^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times q} & \bm{0}_{N \times 2N} \\
        \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \tfrac{1}{q} \bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times 2N} \\
        \bm{0}_{2N \times N} & \bm{0}_{2N \times q} & \bm{0}_{2N \times 2N}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The sample semi-variance in \eqref{eq:empirical_svar} equals $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}^-(\bm{x}_t)\, \bm{x}_t$, where
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{\Sigma}^-(\bm{x}_t) = \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k \,:\, \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}}) < 0}
    (\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}})(\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}})^\top
\end{equation*}
is the sample semicovariance matrix of the observations below the portfolio mean, so the semi-variance and the $L^2$
penalty together equal $\bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{\Sigma}^-(\bm{x}_t) + \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N) \bm{x}_t$. The penalty shrinks this
semicovariance matrix toward a scaled identity matrix in the same way as it does for the variance. 
The portfolio optimization problem under semi-variance
then becomes a quadratic program with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:svar_reg_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t + \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top \\
    \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times q} & \bm{I}_N & -\bm{I}_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \bm{0}_{q \times N} \\
    \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     u_k \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforces the budget constraint and the remaining $N$ rows enforce the weight
decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp}. The first $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ encode the inequality constraints
\eqref{eq:svar_slack}, extended with zeros for the new variables, and the final row of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ imposes the
leverage constraint \eqref{eq:leverage_constraint}.

\section{Sequential Optimization} \label{app:mv_separate}

As we have considered for Expected Shortfall in \Cref{app:separate-optimization}, constructing an international portfolio also requires
selecting currency exposures. In the sequential portfolio optimization approach, the asset weights and currency
exposures are optimized in two steps. First, the asset weights $\bm{x}_t$ are optimized on the fully hedged returns,
then, conditional on the optimal $\bm{x}_t^*$, the currency exposures $\bm{\psi}_t \in \mathbb{R}^M$ are optimized. The
two optimization steps are given by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_sep_asset_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \rho\biggl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} \biggr)
    + \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,\ \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_sep_curr_obj}
    \bm{\psi}_t^* \mid \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\psi}_t} \left\{
    \rho\biggl( \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t) \biggr)
    + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The regularization parameters are kept separate across assets ($\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$)
and currencies ($\lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c$).

The fully hedged return vector $\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}$, defined in \Cref{sec:matrix-representation}, replaces
the asset return vector $\bm{R}_{t+1}$ of Sections~\ref{app:mv_definitions}--\ref{app:mv_regularization}, with
expected value $\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and conditional covariance matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{\Sigma}^a_t = \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ (\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} - \bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)(\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} - \bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)^\top \bigr]
    \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N},
\end{equation*}
which in the programs is the Ledoit--Wolf estimator of \Cref{chap:optimization-framework}. The asset step
\eqref{eq:mv_sep_asset_obj} is therefore identical to the regularized programs \eqref{eq:var_reg_qp} for variance and
\eqref{eq:svar_reg_qp} for semi-variance, and is obtained from them by substituting $\bm{\Sigma}^a_t$ for
$\bm{\Sigma}_t$ in \eqref{eq:var_reg_qp}, and the demeaned matrix $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}$ of the historically sampled fully hedged returns
$\bm{R}^{fh} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ for $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}}$ in \eqref{eq:svar_reg_qp}.

In the currency step \eqref{eq:mv_sep_curr_obj}, the asset weights $\bm{x}_t^*$ are fixed and the currency exposures
$\bm{\psi}_t$ are optimized. The currency exposure $\psi_{c,t}$ is the net
exposure of the portfolio to the foreign currency $c$ as defined in \Cref{sec:matrix-representation}, and each unit of
net exposure earns the excess currency return $e_{c,t+1} - f_{c,t}$. The currency step has no budget constraint, since
the currency overlay is a zero-investment portfolio whose domestic exposure $\psi_{0,t} = -\bm{1}_M^\top \bm{\psi}_t$ is
inferred residually, and no leverage constraint is imposed on the currency exposures. We linearize the currency $L^1$
penalty with the decomposition $\psi_{c,t} = \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ - \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^-$ as in \Cref{app:l1}, and write
the currency $L^2$ penalty as a quadratic form as in \Cref{app:l2}.

\subsection{Currency Overlay under Variance} \label{app:mv_var_sep_curr}

Let $\bm{\mu}^c_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^M$ denote the expected excess currency
return, and
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
    \bm{\Sigma}^c_t &= \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t - \bm{\mu}^c_t)
    (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t - \bm{\mu}^c_t)^\top \bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times M}, \\
    \bm{\Sigma}^{ac}_t &= \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ (\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} - \bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)
    (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t - \bm{\mu}^c_t)^\top \bigr] \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times M}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
the conditional covariance matrix of the excess currency returns and their conditional cross-covariance matrix with
the fully hedged returns. In the programs, $\bm{\Sigma}^c_t$ is the Ledoit--Wolf estimator and $\bm{\Sigma}^{ac}_t$ the
sample cross-covariance matrix of \Cref{chap:optimization-framework}. The variance of the hedged portfolio return
decomposes as
\begin{equation*}
    \text{Var}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t) \bigr)
    = \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{\Sigma}^a_t \bm{x}_t^*
    + 2\, \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{\Sigma}^{ac}_t \bm{\psi}_t
    + \bm{\psi}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}^c_t \bm{\psi}_t,
\end{equation*}
where the first term does not depend on $\bm{\psi}_t$ and is subsequently dropped from the currency step. The decision vector of
the currency step is
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top
    \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3M},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2\, \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{\Sigma}^{ac}_t,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \bm{\Sigma}^c_t + \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times 2M} \\
        \bm{0}_{2M \times M} & \bm{0}_{2M \times 2M}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The currency overlay optimization under variance then becomes a quadratic program with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:var_sep_curr_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_M & \bm{I}_M & -\bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \bm{0}_M, \\[4pt]
    \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^- \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } c = 1, \dots, M.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $M$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} for the currency exposures, and
the fixed asset weights are present through the linear term $2\, (\bm{\Sigma}^{ac}_t)^\top \bm{x}_t^*$ in $\tilde{\bm{c}}$.

\subsection{Currency Overlay under Semi-Variance} \label{app:mv_svar_sep_curr}

For semi-variance, we use the empirical approximation of \Cref{app:mv_svar_qp} for the hedged portfolio return. As
in \Cref{app:separate-optimization}, let $\bm{R}^{fh}_k$ denote the $k$-th sampled fully hedged return vector, and
$\bm{e}_k$ and $\bm{f}_k$ the $k$-th sampled currency return and forward premium vectors. We stack the sampled excess
currency returns $(\bm{e}_k - \bm{f}_k)^\top$ row by row into the matrix $\bm{R}^c \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times M}$, and
collect the fixed asset-portfolio returns in $\bm{R}^{fh}_P = \bm{R}^{fh} \bm{x}_t^* \in \mathbb{R}^q$. Since the
sample mean of the hedged portfolio returns $\bm{R}^{fh}_P + \bm{R}^c \bm{\psi}_t$ is linear in $\bm{\psi}_t$, their
demeaned formulation is simply $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P + \mathring{\bm{R}}^c \bm{\psi}_t$, where
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P = \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh} \bm{x}_t^*$. Introducing the auxiliary variables
$\bm{u}_\psi \ge \bm{0}_q$, the slack constraint \eqref{eq:svar_slack} becomes
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{u}_\psi \ge -\mathring{\bm{R}}^c \bm{\psi}_t - \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    -\mathring{\bm{R}}^c \bm{\psi}_t - \bm{u}_\psi \le \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P.
\end{equation*}
The decision vector of the currency step is
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \bm{u}_\psi^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3M+q},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_M^\top,& \bm{0}_q^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times q} & \bm{0}_{M \times 2M} \\
        \bm{0}_{q \times M} & \tfrac{1}{q} \bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times 2M} \\
        \bm{0}_{2M \times M} & \bm{0}_{2M \times q} & \bm{0}_{2M \times 2M}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The currency overlay optimization under semi-variance then becomes a quadratic program with linear constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:svar_sep_curr_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times q} & \bm{I}_M & -\bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \bm{0}_M, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^c & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times M} & \bm{0}_{q \times M} \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P, \\[4pt]
    u_{k,\psi} \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } c = 1, \dots, M,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $M$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:l1_decomp} for the currency exposures,
and the $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ encode the slack constraints of the currency step.
\section{Joint Optimization} \label{app:mv_joint}

In the joint portfolio optimization approach, asset and currency weights are optimized
simultaneously rather than sequentially. We stack them into the combined decision vector
$\bm{\theta}_t = [\bm{x}_t^\top, \bm{\psi}_t^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$ and the combined
return vector
$\bm{R}_{t+1} = [\bm{R}^{fh\top}_{t+1},\, (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t)^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$,
with joint mean $\bm{\mu} = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$ and joint
covariance $\bm{\Sigma} = \mathrm{Var}_t(\bm{R}_{t+1}) \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+M) \times (N+M)}$.
The joint optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_joint_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{\theta}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\theta}_t} \left\{ -\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{\mu}
    + \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\, \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}\, \bm{\theta}_t
    + \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 + \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 \right\}, \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = 1, \quad \|\bm{L} \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The following matrices and vector adapt the problem to the joint optimization setting
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{q}_{N,M} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N \\ \bm{0}_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \bm{0}_{M \times M} \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{S}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^a \bm{I}_N & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_1^c \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{S}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{\lambda_2^a}\, \bm{I}_N & 0 \\ 0 & \sqrt{\lambda_2^c}\, \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
like in the Expected Shortfall problem \eqref{eq:joint_obj}. The vector
$\bm{q}_{N,M}$ applies the budget constraint to the asset weights only, so that
$\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1$, leaving the currency weights as a
zero-investment overlay. The matrix $\bm{L}$ applies the leverage constraint to the asset
weights only, giving $\|\bm{L}\bm{\theta}_t\|_1 = \|\bm{x}_t\|_1$. The diagonal regularization
matrices $\bm{S}_1$ and $\bm{S}_2$ allow the asset and currency penalties to differ within a
single objective,
\begin{equation*}
    \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 = \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1,
    \qquad
    \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 = \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2.
\end{equation*}

The joint covariance is
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{\Sigma} =
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\Sigma}^a & \bm{\Sigma}^{ac} \\ (\bm{\Sigma}^{ac})^\top & \bm{\Sigma}^c \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
with the asset, currency, and cross-covariance matrices $\bm{\Sigma}^a$, $\bm{\Sigma}^c$, and
$\bm{\Sigma}^{ac}$ defined as in \Cref{app:mv_separate}. The portfolio variance then expands as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_joint_var}
    \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}\, \bm{\theta}_t
    = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}^a \bm{x}_t
    + 2\, \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}^{ac} \bm{\psi}_t
    + \bm{\psi}_t^\top \bm{\Sigma}^c \bm{\psi}_t.
\end{equation}
This is the same decomposition that appears in the currency overlay step
\eqref{eq:mv_curr_expanded}. There, however, the asset weights are fixed at $\bm{x}_t^*$, so
the first term is constant and drops from the objective, while the cross term simplifies to 
the linear form $\gamma\, \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{\Sigma}^{ac} \bm{\psi}_t$. In the joint
problem both $\bm{x}_t$ and $\bm{\psi}_t$ are free. This is how thejoint approach captures the
asset--currency dependence.

The joint mean $\bm{\mu}$ and covariance $\bm{\Sigma}$ are the Bayes--Stein and Ledoit--Wolf
estimators of \Cref{app:estimators}, here applied to the combined return vector
$\bm{R}_{t+1}$. Because the Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage acts on the full combined covariance, the
cross-covariance matrix $\bm{\Sigma}^{ac}$ is shrunk together with the asset and currency matrices. This is
in contrast to the separate approach, where the cross-covariance enters the currency overlay
only through the fixed asset weights $\bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{\Sigma}^{ac}$ and is not
shrunk \citep{SparseAndStableInternationalPortfolioOptimization}.

Applying the $L_1$ decomposition to all $N+M$ components of $\bm{\theta}_t$,
and collecting the asset and currency parts separately, we form the augmented vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{\psi}_t^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+3M}.
\end{equation*}
The cost vector and penalty matrix are
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix}\, -\bm{\mu}^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \,\end{bmatrix}^\top,
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \gamma \bm{\Sigma}^a + 2\lambda_2^a \bm{I}_N & \gamma \bm{\Sigma}^{ac} & \bm{0}_{N \times (2N+2M)} \\
        \gamma (\bm{\Sigma}^{ac})^\top & \gamma \bm{\Sigma}^c + 2\lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times (2N+2M)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+2M) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M) \times (2N+2M)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The active block of $\tilde{\bm{Q}}$ combines the joint covariance with the $L_2$ penalty as
$\gamma \bm{\Sigma} + 2 \bm{S}_2^2$, where the off-diagonal $\gamma \bm{\Sigma}^{ac}$ couples the
asset and currency weights. The joint portfolio
optimization problem is then the quadratic program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mv_joint_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\, \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top \\
    \bm{I}_{N+M} & \bm{I}_{N+M} & -\bm{I}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    \le b = \ell, \\[6pt]
    \Delta_{x,i,t}^\pm &\ge 0, \quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^\pm \ge 0,
    \quad \text{for}\ i = 1, \dots, N, \ c = 1, \dots, M.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In $\tilde{\bm{D}}$, the first row enforces the asset budget constraint
$\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1$, and the remaining $N+M$ rows enforce the weight decomposition
\eqref{eq:l1_decomp} for all components of $\bm{\theta}_t$. The single row of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$
imposes the asset-only leverage constraint \eqref{eq:leverage_constraint}, giving
$\bm{1}_N^\top(\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+ + \bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-) = \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell$.

The joint formulation solves a single quadratic program over $\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t$,
capturing the interaction between asset and currency exposures through the
cross-covariance matrix $\bm{\Sigma}^{ac}$ in $\tilde{\bm{Q}}$. Compared with the joint Expected
Shortfall program \eqref{eq:joint_qp}, the mean-variance program is again smaller
its decision vector has dimension $3N+3M$ rather than $3N+3M+q+1$, and its only inequality is the leverage constraint.
As in the Expected Shortfall case, the joint program is of higher dimension than the two separate
programs of \Cref{app:mv_separate}.

All quadratic programs derived in this appendix share the same convex structure and are
solvable with the same quadratic programming solver. Each extension of the program, from the
base regularized single-currency formulation \eqref{eq:mv_reg_qp} to the joint multi-currency
program \eqref{eq:mv_joint_qp}, modifies only the covariance and cost matrices and the constraint
blocks that encode the budget, leverage, and weight decomposition, without changing the program
structure.
