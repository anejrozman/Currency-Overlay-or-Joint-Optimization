\chapter{Optimization Derivation: Mean Absolute Deviation} \label{app:mad_derivation}

In this appendix, we derive the optimization problems used in the main text for portfolio
optimization under the mean absolute deviation (MAD) risk measure.
We begin by defining the MAD risk risk measure and formulate the base portoflio 
optimization problem. We then introduce regularization techniques and extend the 
formulation to the multi-currency setting, considering sequential overlay optimization 
and joint optimization approaches. Finally, we discuss the implementation of 
the optimization problems in Python using the CVXPY library. 

\section{Definition and Representation for Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:mad_representation}

We define the mean absolute deviation of the portfolio return, which Konno and Yamazaki \cite{KonnoYamazaki1991}
propose as an alternative to variance for portfolio optimization. Unlike variance, mean absolute deviation requires no
estimate of the covariance matrix and can be implemented efficiently as a linear program instead of a quadratic program. 

Let $\bm{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote the portfolio weights, $\bm{R}_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the asset return vector
and $\bm{\mu}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the expected asset return. The mean absolute deviation
of the portfolio return is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_def}
    \text{MAD}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    = \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ \lvert \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\mu}_t - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \rvert \bigr].
\end{equation}
The inner map $\bm{x}_t \mapsto \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{\mu}_t - \bm{R}_{t+1})$ is linear and the function
$z \mapsto \lvert z \rvert$ is convex. The composition of a convex function with an affine map is convex
\cite[Section~3.2.2]{boyd2004convex}. Expectation is a linear operator which preserves convexity, so the mean absolute
deviation is convex in $\bm{x}_t$. Hence any local minimum is global, ensuring that the portfolio optimization problem
under mean absolute deviation can be solved reliably with standard convex optimization methods.

The portfolio optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_risk_min}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \text{MAD}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr),
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where the budget constraint enforces full investment. Let $\bm{y}_1, \dots, \bm{y}_q \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote $q$
historical realizations of the asset return vector $\bm{R}_{t+1}$. As for semi-variance in \Cref{app:mv_svar_qp}, we
approximate the expected asset return $\bm{\mu}_t$ by the sample mean
$\bar{\bm{y}} = \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \bm{y}_k$ and the conditional expectation in \eqref{eq:mad_def} by the
empirical average over this sample, so that the portfolio optimization problem under mean absolute deviation becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:empirical_mad}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q
    \lvert \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k \rvert,
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1.
\end{equation}
Each summand is convex but non-linear. Since the deviations $\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k$ sum
to zero over the sample, the sum of their positive parts equals the sum of their negative parts, and the absolute value
of each deviation is the sum of its two parts. The empirical mean absolute deviation is therefore twice the average
shortfall of the portfolio return below its sample mean \cite{Speranza1993, OgryczakRuszczynski1999}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_identity}
    \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \lvert \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k \rvert
    = \frac{2}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+.
\end{equation}
We linearize the right-hand side by introducing, for each observation $k$, an auxiliary variable $u_k \ge 0$ subject
to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_slack}
    u_k \ge \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}}) + u_k \ge 0.
\end{equation}
The objective becomes
$\frac{2}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q u_k = \frac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top \bm{u}$. It is strictly increasing in each $u_k$ and
minimized, so every $u_k$ is driven to its lower bound at the optimum,
$u_k^* = (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+$. The portfolio optimization problem therefore has the
constraints of the semi-variance program \eqref{eq:svar_base_qp}, with the auxiliary variables entering the objective
linearly.

By writing the decision variables in the vector
$\tilde{\bm{x}}_t = [\bm{x}_t^\top, \bm{u}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q}$ and the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{c} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top, & \dfrac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q},
\end{equation*}
the portfolio optimization problem \eqref{eq:empirical_mad} can be written as a linear program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_base_lp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* = \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
     \bm{c}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \bm{D} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = E = 1, \\[2pt]
    & \bm{A} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{I}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \le \bm{b} = \bm{0}_q, \\[2pt]
    & u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (N+q)}$ encodes the budget constraint, while
$\bm{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+q)}$ and $\bm{b} \in \mathbb{R}^q$ encode the $q$ inequality constraints
\eqref{eq:mad_slack}. The matrix
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} = \bm{R}^{\text{data}} - \bm{1}_q \bar{\bm{y}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ is the
demeaned sample matrix introduced in \Cref{app:mv_svar_qp}, where $\bm{R}^{\text{data}}$ is comprised of the sampled
return vectors $\bm{y}_k^\top$ row by row, so that the $k$-th row of $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}}$ is
$(\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}})^\top$. This formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under
mean absolute deviation.

\section{Regularization} \label{app:mad_regularization}

The base problem \eqref{eq:mad_base_lp} is known to be unstable in high-dimensional settings, where the portfolio weights
can fluctuate strongly from sample to sample \cite{kondor2007noise}. As for the other risk measures in \Cref{app:es_derivation,app:mv_derivation}, we stabilize it with
the three regularization techniques of Sections~\ref{app:l1}--\ref{app:leverage}, an $L^1$ penalty
$\lambda_1 \|\bm{x}_t\|_1$, an $L^2$ penalty $\lambda_2 \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2$ and an asset leverage constraint
$\|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a$, with $\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \ge 0$ and $\ell^a \ge 1$. Splitting the weights into their
positive and negative parts gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_l1_decomp}
    \bm{x}_t = \bm{\Delta}_t^+ - \bm{\Delta}_t^-, \qquad
    \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 = \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-) \le \ell^a, \qquad
    \bm{\Delta}_t^+, \bm{\Delta}_t^- \ge \bm{0}_N,
\end{equation}
where the identity for $\|\bm{x}_t\|_1$ holds at the optimum, so that the $L^1$ penalty becomes
$\lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-)$ and the $L^2$ penalty is the quadratic form
$\bm{x}_t^\top (\lambda_2 \bm{I}_N) \bm{x}_t$.

Adding the three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:mad_base_lp} extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{u}^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top,& \tfrac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times (2N+q)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+q) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+q) \times (2N+q)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The portfolio optimization problem under mean absolute deviation then becomes a quadratic program with linear
constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_reg_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t + \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top \\
    \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times q} & \bm{I}_N & -\bm{I}_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \bm{0}_{q \times N} \\
    \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     u_k \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforces the budget constraint and the remaining $N$ rows enforce the weight
decomposition \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}. The first $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ encode the inequality constraints
\eqref{eq:mad_slack}, extended with zeros for the new variables, and the final row of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ imposes the
leverage constraint \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}.
