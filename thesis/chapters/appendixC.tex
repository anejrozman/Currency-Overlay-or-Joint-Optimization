\chapter{Optimization Derivation: Mean Absolute Deviation} \label{app:mad_derivation}

In this appendix, we derive the portfolio optimization problems used in the main text, building up from the definition
of the mean absolute deviation to the programs solved in the empirical analysis. In \Cref{app:mad_representation} we
define the mean absolute deviation, show that it is convex in the portfolio weights, and write the portfolio
optimization problem as a linear program. In \Cref{app:mad_regularization} we regularize the program by adding an
$L^1$ penalty, an $L^2$ penalty, and a leverage constraint. We then extend the program to the multi-currency setting,
for sequential optimization in \Cref{app:mad_separate} and for joint optimization in \Cref{app:mad_joint}. Finally, in
\Cref{app:mad_mean_risk} we add the expected portfolio return to the objectives of the sequential and joint problems.

\section{Definition and Representation for Portfolio Optimization} \label{app:mad_representation}

We define the mean absolute deviation of the portfolio return, which Konno and Yamazaki \cite{KonnoYamazaki1991}
propose as an alternative to variance for portfolio optimization. Unlike variance, mean absolute deviation requires no
estimate of the covariance matrix and can be implemented efficiently as a linear program instead of a quadratic program. 

Let $\bm{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote the portfolio weights, $\bm{R}_{t+1} \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the asset return vector,
and $\bm{\mu}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N$ the expected asset return. The mean absolute deviation
of the portfolio return is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_def}
    \text{MAD}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    = \mathbb{E}_t\bigl[ \lvert \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{\mu}_t - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \rvert \bigr].
\end{equation}
The inner map $\bm{x}_t \mapsto \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{\mu}_t - \bm{R}_{t+1})$ is linear and the function
$z \mapsto \lvert z \rvert$ is convex. The composition of a convex function with an affine map is convex
\cite[Section~3.2.2]{boyd2004convex}. Expectation is a linear operator which preserves convexity, so the mean absolute
deviation is convex in $\bm{x}_t$. Hence any local minimum is global, ensuring that the portfolio optimization problem
under mean absolute deviation can be solved reliably with standard convex optimization methods.

The portfolio optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_risk_min}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \text{MAD}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr),
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,
\end{equation}
where the budget constraint enforces full investment. Let $\bm{y}_1, \dots, \bm{y}_q \in \mathbb{R}^N$ denote $q$
historical realizations of the asset return vector $\bm{R}_{t+1}$. As for semi-variance in \Cref{app:mv_svar_qp}, we
approximate the expected asset return $\bm{\mu}_t$ by the sample mean
$\bar{\bm{y}} = \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \bm{y}_k$ and the conditional expectation in \eqref{eq:mad_def} by the
empirical average over this sample, so that the portfolio optimization problem under mean absolute deviation becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:empirical_mad}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q
    \lvert \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k \rvert,
    \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1.
\end{equation}
Each summand is convex but non-linear. Since the deviations $\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k$ sum
to zero over the sample, the sum of their positive parts equals the sum of their negative parts, and the absolute value
of each deviation is the sum of its two parts. The empirical mean absolute deviation is therefore twice the average
shortfall of the portfolio return below its sample mean \cite{Speranza1993, OgryczakRuszczynski1999}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_identity}
    \frac{1}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q \lvert \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k \rvert
    = \frac{2}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+.
\end{equation}
We linearize the right-hand side by introducing, for each observation $k$, an auxiliary variable $u_k \ge 0$ subject
to
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_slack}
    u_k \ge \bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    \bm{x}_t^\top (\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}}) + u_k \ge 0.
\end{equation}
The objective becomes
$\frac{2}{q} \sum_{k=1}^q u_k = \frac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top \bm{u}$. It is strictly increasing in each $u_k$ and
minimized, so every $u_k$ is driven to its lower bound at the optimum,
$u_k^* = (\bm{x}_t^\top \bar{\bm{y}} - \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{y}_k)^+$. The portfolio optimization problem therefore has the
constraints of the semi-variance program \eqref{eq:svar_base_qp}, with the auxiliary variables entering the objective
linearly.

By writing the decision variables in the vector
$\tilde{\bm{x}}_t = [\bm{x}_t^\top, \bm{u}^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q}$ and the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{c} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top, & \dfrac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+q},
\end{equation*}
the portfolio optimization problem \eqref{eq:empirical_mad} can be written as a linear program
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_base_lp}
\begin{aligned}
    & \ \ \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* = \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
     \bm{c}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    & \bm{D} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = E = 1, \\[2pt]
    & \bm{A} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t =
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{I}_q \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t \le \bm{b} = \bm{0}_q, \\[2pt]
    & u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\bm{D} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (N+q)}$ encodes the budget constraint, while
$\bm{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+q)}$ and $\bm{b} \in \mathbb{R}^q$ encode the $q$ inequality constraints
\eqref{eq:mad_slack}. The matrix
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} = \bm{R}^{\text{data}} - \bm{1}_q \bar{\bm{y}}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ is the
demeaned sample matrix introduced in \Cref{app:mv_svar_qp}, where $\bm{R}^{\text{data}}$ is composed of the sampled
return vectors $\bm{y}_k^\top$ row by row, so that the $k$-th row of $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}}$ is
$(\bm{y}_k - \bar{\bm{y}})^\top$. This formulation serves as the base unregularized portfolio optimization problem under
mean absolute deviation.

\section{Regularization} \label{app:mad_regularization}

The base problem \eqref{eq:mad_base_lp} is known to be unstable in high-dimensional settings, where the portfolio weights
can fluctuate strongly from sample to sample \cite{kondor2007noise}. As for the other risk measures in Appendices~\ref{app:es_derivation} and~\ref{app:mv_derivation}, we stabilize it with
the three regularization techniques of Sections~\ref{app:l1}--\ref{app:leverage}, an $L^1$ penalty
$\lambda_1 \|\bm{x}_t\|_1$, an $L^2$ penalty $\lambda_2 \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2$, and an asset leverage constraint
$\|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a$, with $\lambda_1, \lambda_2 \ge 0$ and $\ell^a \ge 1$. Splitting the weights into their
positive and negative parts gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_l1_decomp}
    \bm{x}_t = \bm{\Delta}_t^+ - \bm{\Delta}_t^-, \qquad
    \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 = \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-) \le \ell^a, \qquad
    \bm{\Delta}_t^+, \bm{\Delta}_t^- \ge \bm{0}_N,
\end{equation}
where the identity for $\|\bm{x}_t\|_1$ holds at the optimum, so that the $L^1$ penalty becomes
$\lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-)$ and the $L^2$ penalty is the quadratic form
$\bm{x}_t^\top (\lambda_2 \bm{I}_N) \bm{x}_t$.

Adding the three regularization terms to the base problem \eqref{eq:mad_base_lp} extends the decision vector to
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{u}^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_t^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+q},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_N^\top,& \tfrac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1 \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2 \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times (2N+q)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+q) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+q) \times (2N+q)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The portfolio optimization problem under mean absolute deviation then becomes a quadratic program with linear
constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_reg_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{x}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{x}}_t + \tilde{\bm{x}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{x}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_N^\top \\
    \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times q} & \bm{I}_N & -\bm{I}_N \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_N \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}} & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times N} & \bm{0}_{q \times N} \\
    \bm{0}_N^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top & \bm{1}_N^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{x}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
     u_k \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{i,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{i,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first row of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforces the budget constraint and the remaining $N$ rows enforce the weight
decomposition \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}. The first $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ encode the inequality constraints
\eqref{eq:mad_slack}, extended with zeros for the new variables, and the final row of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ imposes the
leverage constraint \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}.

\section{Sequential Optimization} \label{app:mad_separate}

As for the other risk measures in Sections~\ref{app:separate-optimization} and~\ref{app:mv_separate}, we consider  an
international portfolio optimization problem that also requires selecting currency exposures. In the sequential portfolio optimization approach,
the asset weights and currency exposures are optimized in two steps. First, the asset weights $\bm{x}_t$ are optimized
on the fully hedged returns. Then, conditional on the optimal $\bm{x}_t^*$, the currency exposures
$\bm{\psi}_t \in \mathbb{R}^M$ are optimized. The two optimization steps are given by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_sep_asset_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \text{MAD}_t\biggl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} \biggr)
    + \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,\ \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_sep_curr_obj}
    \bm{\psi}_t^* \mid \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\psi}_t} \left\{
    \text{MAD}_t\biggl( \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t) \biggr)
    + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The regularization parameters are kept separate across assets ($\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$) and currencies
($\lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c$).

The fully hedged return vector $\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}$, defined in \Cref{sec:matrix-representation}, takes the place of
the asset return vector $\bm{R}_{t+1}$ of Sections~\ref{app:mad_representation}--\ref{app:mad_regularization}, so the
asset step \eqref{eq:mad_sep_asset_obj} is the regularized program \eqref{eq:mad_reg_qp} with the demeaned matrix
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}$ of the historically sampled fully hedged returns $\bm{R}^{fh} \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times N}$ in
place of $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{data}}$.

In the currency step \eqref{eq:mad_sep_curr_obj}, the asset weights $\bm{x}_t^*$ are fixed and the currency exposures
$\bm{\psi}_t$ are optimized. The currency step has no budget constraint, since the currency overlay is a zero-investment
portfolio whose domestic exposure $\psi_{0,t} = -\bm{1}_M^\top \bm{\psi}_t$ is inferred residually, and no leverage
constraint is imposed on the currency exposures. We split the currency exposures as
$\bm{\psi}_t = \bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+ - \bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-$ with $\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+, \bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^- \ge
\bm{0}_M$ as in \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}, which linearizes the currency $L^1$ penalty, and write the currency $L^2$
penalty as the quadratic form $\bm{\psi}_t^\top (\lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M) \bm{\psi}_t$.

For the empirical approximation of the currency step, let $\bm{R}^{fh}_k$ denote the $k$-th sampled fully hedged
return vector, and $\bm{e}_k$ and $\bm{f}_k$ the $k$-th sampled currency return and forward premium vectors. We stack
the sampled excess currency returns $(\bm{e}_k - \bm{f}_k)^\top$ row by row into the matrix
$\bm{R}^c \in \mathbb{R}^{q \times M}$ and collect the fixed asset-portfolio returns in
$\bm{R}^{fh}_P = \bm{R}^{fh} \bm{x}_t^* \in \mathbb{R}^q$. Since the sample mean is linear in the returns, the
demeaned hedged portfolio returns are $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P + \mathring{\bm{R}}^c \bm{\psi}_t$, as for
semi-variance in \Cref{app:mv_svar_sep_curr}, where $\mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P = \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh} \bm{x}_t^*$ and
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^c$ is the demeaned matrix of the sampled excess currency returns. These demeaned returns also sum to
zero, so the identity \eqref{eq:mad_identity} applies, and introducing the auxiliary variables
$\bm{u}_\psi \ge \bm{0}_q$, the slack constraint \eqref{eq:mad_slack} becomes
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{u}_\psi \ge -\mathring{\bm{R}}^c \bm{\psi}_t - \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    -\mathring{\bm{R}}^c \bm{\psi}_t - \bm{u}_\psi \le \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P,
\end{equation*}
with the objective $\frac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top \bm{u}_\psi$.

The decision vector of the currency step is
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \bm{u}_\psi^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{3M+q},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_M^\top,& \tfrac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times (2M+q)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2M+q) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2M+q) \times (2M+q)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The currency overlay optimization under mean absolute deviation then becomes a quadratic program with linear
constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_sep_curr_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t} \quad
     \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M \times q} & \bm{I}_M & -\bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \bm{0}_M, \\[4pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^c & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times M} & \bm{0}_{q \times M} \end{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\bm{\psi}}_t \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \mathring{\bm{R}}^{fh}_P, \\[4pt]
    u_{k,\psi} \ge 0, & \quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^- \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^+ \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } c = 1, \dots, M,\ k = 1, \dots, q.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The $M$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{D}}$ enforce the weight decomposition \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp} for the currency exposures,
and the $q$ rows of $\tilde{\bm{A}}$ encode the slack constraints of the currency step.

\section{Joint Optimization} \label{app:mad_joint}

In the joint portfolio optimization approach, the asset weights and currency exposures are optimized simultaneously
rather than sequentially. As for the other risk measures in Sections~\ref{app:joint-optimization}
and~\ref{app:mv_joint}, we stack the weights into the combined decision vector
$\bm{\theta}_t = [\bm{x}_t^\top, \bm{\psi}_t^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$ and the corresponding returns into the
combined return vector
$\bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} = [\bm{R}^{fh\top}_{t+1},\, (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t)^\top]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$,
so that $\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} = \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}
+ \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t)$ is the hedged portfolio return of \Cref{app:mad_separate}.
The joint optimization problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_joint_obj}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{\theta}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\theta}_t} \left\{ \text{MAD}_t\biggl( \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} \biggr)
    + \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 + \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = 1, \quad \|\bm{L}^a \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{q}_{N,M} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{1}_N \\ \bm{0}_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{L}^a = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \bm{0}_{M \times M} \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{S}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^a \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \lambda_1^c \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \bm{S}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} \sqrt{\lambda_2^a}\, \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \sqrt{\lambda_2^c}\, \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The vector $\bm{q}_{N,M}$ and the matrix $\bm{L}^a$ apply the budget and leverage constraints to the asset weights
only, and $\bm{S}_1$ and $\bm{S}_2$ encode the asset and currency penalties. We decompose
$\bm{\theta}_t = \bm{\Delta}_t^+ - \bm{\Delta}_t^-$ as in \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}, with
$\bm{\Delta}_t^\pm = [(\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^\pm)^\top, (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^\pm)^\top]^\top \ge \bm{0}_{N+M}$, which
linearizes $\|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1$ and the leverage constraint
$\|\bm{L}^a \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 = \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top (\bm{\Delta}_t^+ + \bm{\Delta}_t^-) \le \ell^a$, and write the $L^2$
penalty as the quadratic form $\bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{S}_2^2\, \bm{\theta}_t$.

For the empirical approximation, let $\bm{R}^{\text{all}} = [\, \bm{R}^{fh}\ \ \bm{R}^c \,] \in
\mathbb{R}^{q \times (N+M)}$ denote the matrix of jointly sampled asset and currency returns, with $\bm{R}^{fh}$ and
$\bm{R}^c$ of \Cref{app:mad_separate}. Since the sample mean is linear in the returns, the demeaned joint returns are
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{all}} \bm{\theta}_t$, as for semi-variance in \Cref{app:mv_svar_joint}, where
$\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{all}}$ is the demeaned matrix of the jointly sampled returns. These demeaned returns sum to
zero, so the identity \eqref{eq:mad_identity} applies, and introducing the auxiliary variables $\bm{u} \ge \bm{0}_q$,
the slack constraint \eqref{eq:mad_slack} becomes
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{u} \ge -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{all}} \bm{\theta}_t
    \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad
    \mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{all}} \bm{\theta}_t + \bm{u} \ge \bm{0}_q,
\end{equation*}
with the objective $\frac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top \bm{u}$.

The decision vector is
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{x}_t^\top,& \bm{\psi}_t^\top,& \bm{u}^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^-)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^-)^\top,&
    (\bm{\Delta}_{x,t}^+)^\top,& (\bm{\Delta}_{\psi,t}^+)^\top \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
    \in \mathbb{R}^{3N+3M+q},
\end{equation*}
with the constants in the vector
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{c}} = \begin{bmatrix}\, \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top,& \tfrac{2}{q} \bm{1}_q^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top,&
    \lambda_1^a \bm{1}_N^\top,& \lambda_1^c \bm{1}_M^\top \,\end{bmatrix}^\top
\end{equation*}
and the penalty matrix
\begin{equation*}
    \tilde{\bm{Q}} =
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \lambda_2^a \bm{I}_N & \bm{0}_{N \times M} & \bm{0}_{N\times(2N + 2M + q)} \\
        \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \lambda_2^c \bm{I}_M & \bm{0}_{M\times(2N + 2M + q)} \\
        \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q) \times N} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q) \times M} & \bm{0}_{(2N+2M+q) \times (2N+2M+q)}
    \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
The joint portfolio optimization problem under mean absolute deviation then becomes a quadratic program with linear
constraints
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_joint_qp}
\begin{aligned}
    \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^* &= \argmin_{\tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{c}}^\top \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t + \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t^\top \tilde{\bm{Q}}\, \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t, \\[2pt]
    \text{s.t.} \quad
    \tilde{\bm{D}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top \\
    \bm{I}_{N+M} & \bm{0}_{(N+M) \times q} & \bm{I}_{N+M} & -\bm{I}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    = \tilde{\bm{E}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \bm{0}_{N+M} \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    \tilde{\bm{A}} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t &=
    \begin{bmatrix} -\mathring{\bm{R}}^{\text{all}} & -\bm{I}_q & \bm{0}_{q \times (N+M)} & \bm{0}_{q \times (N+M)} \\
    \bm{0}_{N+M}^\top & \bm{0}_q^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top & \bm{q}_{N,M}^\top \end{bmatrix} \tilde{\bm{\theta}}_t
    \le \tilde{\bm{b}} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_q \\ \ell^a \end{bmatrix}, \\[6pt]
    & \quad u_k \ge 0 \quad \text{for } k = 1, \dots, q, \\[2pt]
    & \quad \Delta_{x,i,t}^\pm \ge 0,\quad \Delta_{\psi,c,t}^\pm \ge 0
    \quad \text{for } i = 1, \dots, N,\ c = 1, \dots, M.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In $\tilde{\bm{D}}$, the first row enforces the asset budget constraint and the remaining $N+M$ rows the weight
decomposition \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp} for all components of $\bm{\theta}_t$. In $\tilde{\bm{A}}$, the first $q$ rows
encode the slack constraints, and the final row imposes the asset-only leverage constraint \eqref{eq:mad_l1_decomp}.

\section{Mean-Risk Optimization} \label{app:mad_mean_risk}

The programs of Sections~\ref{app:mad_representation}--\ref{app:mad_joint} minimize the risk measure only. As for the
other risk measures in Sections~\ref{app:mean-risk} and~\ref{app:mv_mean_risk}, a mean-risk program subtracts the
expected portfolio return, scaled by $1/\gamma$, from the risk measure, where $\gamma > 0$ is the risk aversion
coefficient. For problem \eqref{eq:mad_risk_min}, this gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_mean_obj}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \text{MAD}_t\bigl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}_{t+1} \bigr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, \bm{\mu}_t^\top \bm{x}_t \right\}, \quad \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1.
\end{equation}

Let $\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}] \in \mathbb{R}^N$ and
$\bm{\mu}^c_t = \mathbb{E}_t[\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^M$ denote the expected fully hedged asset returns and
the expected excess currency returns, and $\bm{\mu}^{\text{all}}_t = [(\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)^\top, (\bm{\mu}^c_t)^\top]^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^{N+M}$. The asset means are estimated from the equally weighted return of each economy, whose mean is
shrunk toward zero with a James--Stein estimator, and the currency means are set to the negative forward premium
$-\bm{f}_t$. Both estimators are defined and further discussed in \Cref{chap:optimization-framework}.

In the sequential approach, the asset step and the currency step become
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_mean_sep_asset}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{x}_t} \left\{ \text{MAD}_t\biggl( \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} \biggr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, (\bm{\mu}^{fh}_t)^\top \bm{x}_t
    + \lambda_1^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^a \|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1,\ \|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_mean_sep_curr}
\begin{gathered}
    \begin{aligned}
    \bm{\psi}_t^* \mid \bm{x}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\psi}_t} \biggl\{ &
    \text{MAD}_t\biggl( \bm{x}_t^{*\top} \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\top (\bm{e}_{t+1} - \bm{f}_t) \biggr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, (\bm{\mu}^c_t)^\top \bm{\psi}_t \\
    & + \lambda_1^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 + \lambda_2^c \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_2^2 \biggr\}
    \end{aligned} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1 \le \ell^c.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
In the joint approach, problem \eqref{eq:mad_joint_obj} becomes
\begin{equation} \label{eq:mad_mean_joint}
\begin{gathered}
    \bm{\theta}_t^* = \argmin_{\bm{\theta}_t} \left\{ \text{MAD}_t\biggl( \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{R}^{\text{all}}_{t+1} \biggr)
    - \frac{1}{\gamma}\, (\bm{\mu}^{\text{all}}_t)^\top \bm{\theta}_t
    + \|\bm{S}_1 \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 + \|\bm{S}_2 \bm{\theta}_t\|_2^2 \right\} \\[4pt]
    \text{s.t. } \bm{\theta}_t^\top \bm{q}_{N,M} = 1, \quad \|\bm{L}^a \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell^a, \quad
    \|\bm{L}^c \bm{\theta}_t\|_1 \le \ell^c,
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
    \bm{L}^c = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{0}_{N \times N} & \bm{0}_{N \times M} \\ \bm{0}_{M \times N} & \bm{I}_M \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}
applies the leverage constraint to the currency exposures only. We add the currency leverage constraint to both the
currency step and the joint program, since without it, the mean term can push the currency exposures toward very large
positions.
