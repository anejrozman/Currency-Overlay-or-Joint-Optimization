\section{Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis} \label{app:hyperparameter-sensitivity}

The regularized portfolio optimization problems include four regularization parameters, the asset penalties
$\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a$ and the currency penalties $\lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c$. These
are selected by grid search on a validation sample, which requires setting a bounded interval for each. The bounds are not arbitrary. As a penalty
grows, the optimal weights approach a limiting allocation, and beyond some threshold a
further increase leaves the solution essentially unchanged. A grid whose upper bound
lies past that threshold is sufficient, in the sense that extending it further would only increase the computational cost of the grid search. 

To locate this threshold we follow
\cite{RegularizedMultiCurrencyExpectedShortfallPortfolios} and define, for each penalty
$\lambda \in \{\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a, \lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c\}$, an increasing
sequence $(\lambda_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ with $\lambda_0 = 0$. Let $\bm{x}_t(\lambda_n)$
and $\bm{\psi}_t(\lambda_n)$ denote the optimal asset and currency overlay weights when
only $\lambda$ is active, that is, when the remaining three penalties are held at zero.
The marginal effect of an incremental increase in regularization is measured in the
supremum norm 
\begin{align*} \label{eq:marginal-effect}
    d_n^a &= \left\| \bm{x}_t(\lambda_n) - \bm{x}_t(\lambda_{n-1}) \right\|_\infty
    \quad \text{for } \lambda \in \{\lambda_1^a, \lambda_2^a\}, \\
    d_n^c &= \left\| \bm{\psi}_t(\lambda_n) - \bm{\psi}_t(\lambda_{n-1}) \right\|_\infty
    \quad \text{for } \lambda \in \{\lambda_1^c, \lambda_2^c\}.
\end{align*}
 
Tthe two 
penalty types approach their limits in different ways. Under an $L_1$ penalty the
weights move to the corner of the feasible set, at which point the penalty term
becomes constant in the weights and doesnt influence the solution. An $L_2$ penalty instead shrinks the
weights smoothly toward the origin and never reaches a corner, so its marginal
effect decays gradually without ever vanishing. Such behavior is well documented in \cite{getsomething}. For this reason we report alongside
$d_n$ the marginal effect on portfolio exposure, $\|\bm{x}_t\|_1$ for the asset penalties
and $\|\bm{\psi}_t\|_1$ for the currency penalties. Exposure is the economically
interpretable quantity, and is the one that we use to determine the upper bound of the grid for the $L_2$ penalties.

The analysis is done on the first estimation window spanning July 1990 to June 2000. To
account for sampling error, each reported quantity is averaged over 5 bootstraped replicates of the estimation window. The results are reported for the small investable universe.

\Cref{fig:hyperparameter-sensitivity-es-marginal} and
\Cref{fig:hyperparameter-sensitivity-es} report the marginal effect and the portfolio
exposure. The difference between the two penalty types is clearest
in the marginal effect plots. The $L_1$ penalties collapse abruptly near their bound, whereas the $L_2$
penalties decay smoothly throughout, which is why their bounds are determined by the exposure
analysis instead.

For both asset penalties, exposure falls from the leverage limit in the unpenalized
problem to $1$. The budget constraint $\bm{x}_t^\top \bm{1}_N = 1$ implies
$\|\bm{x}_t\|_1 \geq 1$, with equality precisely when the portfolio holds no short
positions. The two penalties reach this floor for different reasons, and only the
$L_1$ penalty ceases to act once it is there. Under $\lambda_1^a$ the penalty term
equals $\|\bm{x}_t\|_1 = 1$ everywhere on the long-only set and is therefore constant
in $\bm{x}_t$, so the solution freezes exactly. Under $\lambda_2^a$ the penalty
$\|\bm{x}_t\|_2^2$ is not constant on that set: exposure stays pinned at its floor
while the weights keep spreading toward the equally weighted portfolio, which is the
limiting allocation as $\lambda_2^a \to \infty$. For the asset $L_2$ penalty we
therefore read the bound as the point at which the short positions have been
eliminated and any further movement is a redistribution within a long-only book.

The overlay weights carry neither a budget nor a leverage constraint, so $\bm{\psi}_t$
shrinks to zero, exactly under $\lambda_1^c$ and asymptotically under $\lambda_2^c$.

On the basis of this analysis we fix the ranges reported in
\Cref{tab:hypergrid-es}. Each range is discretised on a log-spaced grid that includes
the zero endpoint, which corresponds to the unregularized optimization problem.

    \label{fig:sparsity-es}
\end{figure}

Figure \ref{fig:sparsity-es} reports the share of the investable universe held at each rebalance date.

Regularization stabilizes the weight vector conditional on the penalty coefficients, 
but those coefficients are themselves re-selected at every rebalance date. 
The number of active positions is sensitive to this recalibration, an L1-dominant selection
 sets weights to zero and yields a concentrated portfolio, whereas an L2-dominant selection 
 shrinks all weights toward the equally weighted portfolio. 
 The oscillation therefore reflects the validation criterion alternating between these two regimes 
 across quarters, not instability in the optimizer itself. The unregularized problem has no such 
 degree of freedom. Its support is determined primarily by the leverage limit.
