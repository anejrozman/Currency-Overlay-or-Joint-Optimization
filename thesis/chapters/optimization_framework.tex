\chapter{Optimization Framework} 
\label{chap:optimization-framework}

This chapter develops the optimization framework used throughout the thesis, building up from a model of multi-currency portfolio returns to the risk objectives, the regularization that stabilizes them, and the two formulations through which asset and currency decisions are combined. The derivations follow the methodology presented in \cite{SparseAndStableInternationalPortfolioOptimization} and \cite{RegularizedMultiCurrencyExpectedShortfallPortfolios}, use simple returns, and assume that rebalancing occurs at the end of each investment period. We begin in Section~\ref{sec:returns} with a model-free representation of one-period portfolio returns for an investor who holds assets denominated in multiple currencies and can take currency forward positions for hedging, decomposing the unhedged return of a foreign asset into its local, currency, and cross-currency components, introducing the currency-mapping matrix that links assets to their implicit currency exposures, and expressing the hedged portfolio return in terms of net currency exposures.

With the return model in place, we formulate the optimization problem. Section~\ref{sec:mv-formulation} states the classical mean-variance criterion, and Section~\ref{sec:es-formulation} introduces Expected Shortfall as a coherent downside-risk measure, together with the Rockafellar--Uryasev representation that casts its minimization as a tractable linear program. Both are presented as instances of a common mean--risk objective, so that the framework treats the risk measure as a swappable component rather than a fixed choice. Section~\ref{sec:regularization} then introduces the $L_1$ and $L_2$ penalties and the leverage constraint used to stabilize the optimization and improve out-of-sample performance.

Finally, Sections~\ref{sec:separate-optimization} and~\ref{sec:joint-optimization} assemble these elements into the two approaches to multi-currency allocation studied throughout the thesis: the separate (currency overlay) formulation, in which asset and currency decisions are made sequentially, and the joint formulation, in which they are optimized simultaneously. The detailed derivations of the resulting quadratic programs are found in Appendix~\ref{app:es_derivation} and Appendix~\ref{app:mv_derivation}.
 
\section{Model-Free Multi-Currency Portfolio Returns}\label{sec:returns}
\subsection{Unhedged Portfolio Returns}

We consider an international investor who allocates their wealth across $N$ assets denominated in up to $M+1$ currencies. We fix a discrete time index $t\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ and one investment period from $t$ to $t{+}1$. Let the investor's \emph{domestic currency} be indexed by $0$ and let the remaining $M$ foreign currencies be indexed by $\mathcal{C} \coloneqq \{1,2,\dots,M\}$.

Let $P_{i,t}>0$ be the price of asset $i$ at time $t$ in its local currency $c_i \in \mathcal{C}\cup \{0\}$. Define the corresponding \emph{simple local-currency asset return} over $(t,t{+}1]$ as
\begin{equation*}
R_{i,t+1} \coloneqq \frac{P_{i,t+1}-P_{i,t}}{P_{i,t}}.
\end{equation*}
Thus $P_{i,t+1}=P_{i,t}(1+R_{i,t+1})$.

For each foreign currency $c\in\mathcal{C}$, let $S_{c,t} > 0$ denote the exchange rate at time $t$, quoted as units of domestic currency per one unit of foreign currency $c$. The associated \emph{simple currency return} over $(t,t{+}1]$ is
\begin{equation*}
e_{c,t+1} \coloneqq \frac{S_{c,t+1}-S_{c,t}}{S_{c,t}}.
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, $S_{c,t+1} = S_{c,t}(1+e_{c,t+1})$. For the domestic currency, we set $S_{0,t}\equiv 1$ and $e_{0,t+1}\equiv 0$.

The domestic-currency value of holding one unit of asset $i$ at time $t$ is $P_{i,t}\,S_{c_i,t}$. Therefore, the \emph{unhedged domestic-currency simple return} of asset $i$ is
\begin{align}
R^{u}_{i,t+1}
&=
\frac{P_{i,t+1}S_{c_i,t+1} - P_{i,t}S_{c_i,t}}{P_{i,t}S_{c_i,t}}
\nonumber\\
&=
\frac{P_{i,t}(1+R_{i,t+1})\,S_{c_i,t}(1+e_{c_i,t+1}) - P_{i,t}S_{c_i,t}}{P_{i,t}S_{c_i,t}}
\nonumber\\
&=
R_{i,t+1} + e_{c_i,t+1} + R_{i,t+1}\,e_{c_i,t+1}.
\label{eq:unhedged-asset-return}
\end{align}

Let $\bm{x}_t\in\R^N$ be the vector of asset portfolio weights chosen at time $t$ and held until $t{+}1$, where $x_{i,t}$ is the fraction of total portfolio value invested in asset $i$ at time $t$. We impose the standard budget constraint $\bm{1}^\T \bm{x}_t = 1$.

Assuming no rebalancing between times $t$ and $t{+}1$, the domestic-currency portfolio's unhedged return is given by
\begin{equation*}
R^{u}_{\mathcal{P},t+1} = \sum_{i=1}^N x_{i,t}\,R^{u}_{i,t+1}.
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Hedged Portfolio Returns}

To manage currency risk, the investor can take positions in one-period currency forward contracts. For each foreign currency $c\in\mathcal{C}$, let $F_{c,t}$ be the forward rate quoted at time $t$ for settlement at $t{+}1$, in units of domestic currency per unit of foreign currency $c$. We define the \emph{forward premium} by
\begin{equation*}
f_{c,t} \coloneqq \frac{F_{c,t}-S_{c,t}}{S_{c,t}}.
\end{equation*}

The investor hedges by selling foreign currency forward. Let $\upphi_{c,t}$ denote the relative notional of the \emph{short} forward position in currency $c$ at time $t$, expressed as a fraction of total portfolio value, with the convention that $\upphi_{c,t}>0$ corresponds to hedging. Per unit of notional, the short position pays $f_{c,t}-e_{c,t+1}$ in domestic currency over the period. Adding these forward payoffs to the unhedged asset returns, the \emph{hedged portfolio return} is
\begin{equation}
R^{h}_{\mathcal{P},t+1}
=
\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^N x_{i,t}\,R^{u}_{i,t+1}}_{R^{u}_{\mathcal{P},t+1}}
+
\sum_{c=1}^M \upphi_{c,t}\,(f_{c,t}-e_{c,t+1}).
\label{eq:hedged-portfolio-return}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Matrix Representation and Decomposition}

To express the returns in a compact form suitable for optimization, we define the currency incidence matrix $\bm{C}\in\{0,1\}^{N\times M}$ by
\begin{equation*}
C_{i,c} \coloneqq
\begin{cases}
1, & \text{if asset $i$ is denominated in foreign currency $c$},\\
0, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Each row of $\bm{C}$ has at most one nonzero entry, and the rows corresponding to domestic assets are identically zero. This allows us to define the portfolio's \emph{implicit foreign-currency exposure} as
\begin{equation*}
\bm{w}_t \coloneqq \bm{C}^\T \bm{x}_t \in \R^M,
\qquad
w_{c,t} = \sum_{i:\,c_i=c} x_{i,t},
\end{equation*}
such that $w_{c,t}$ is the total portfolio weight invested in assets denominated in currency $c$. The implicit domestic exposure follows residually as $w_{0,t} = 1 - \bm{1}_M^\T \bm{w}_t$, since the asset weights sum to one.

We express returns in vector form $\bm{R}_{t+1} \in \R^N$, $\bm{e}_{t+1} \in \R^M$, and the forward premiums as $\bm{f}_t \in \R^M$. The product $\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}\in\R^N$ aligns currency returns to assets, with $i$th component $e_{c_i,t+1}$ for foreign assets and $0$ for domestic ones. Using \eqref{eq:unhedged-asset-return}, the vector of \emph{unhedged domestic returns} across assets can be written as
\begin{equation*}
\bm{R}^{u}_{t+1}
=
\bm{R}_{t+1}
+
\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}
+
(\bm{R}_{t+1}\had\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}),
\end{equation*}
where $\had$ denotes the Hadamard product. 

Expressing the forward positions in vector form as $\bm{\upphi}_t\in\R^M$, the hedged portfolio return \eqref{eq:hedged-portfolio-return} can be written as
\begin{equation}
R^{h}_{\mathcal{P},t+1} = \bm{x}_t^\T \bm{R}^{u}_{t+1} + \bm{\upphi}_t^\T(\bm{f}_t-\bm{e}_{t+1}).
\label{eq:hedged-vector}
\end{equation}

Setting $\bm{\upphi}_t=\bm{w}_t=\bm{C}^\T\bm{x}_t$ neutralizes the implicit currency exposure. Substituting this together with the unhedged return vector into \eqref{eq:hedged-vector}, the \emph{fully hedged portfolio return} is
\begin{align*}
R^{fh}_{\mathcal{P},t+1}
&= \bm{x}_t^\T\bm{R}^{u}_{t+1} + \big(\bm{C}^\T\bm{x}_t\big)^\T(\bm{f}_t-\bm{e}_{t+1})\\[2pt]
&= \bm{x}_t^\T\Big[\bm{R}_{t+1} + \bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1} + \big(\bm{R}_{t+1}\had\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}\big)\Big] + \bm{x}_t^\T\bm{C}(\bm{f}_t-\bm{e}_{t+1})\\[2pt]
&= \bm{x}_t^\T\Big[\bm{R}_{t+1} + \bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1} + \big(\bm{R}_{t+1}\had\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}\big) + \bm{C}\bm{f}_t - \bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}\Big]\\[2pt]
&= \bm{x}_t^\T\Big[\bm{R}_{t+1} + \bm{C}\bm{f}_t + \big(\bm{R}_{t+1}\had\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}\big)\Big]\\[2pt]
&= \bm{x}_t^\T\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}.
\end{align*}
The first-order currency terms $\pm\,\bm{x}_t^\T\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}$ cancel, leaving the \emph{fully hedged asset return vector}
\begin{equation*}
\bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1}
=
\bm{R}_{t+1}
+
\bm{C}\bm{f}_t
+
(\bm{R}_{t+1}\had\bm{C}\bm{e}_{t+1}).
\end{equation*}

More generally, define the \emph{net currency exposure} $\bm{\psi}_t \coloneqq \bm{w}_t-\bm{\upphi}_t$. Imposing $\sum_{c=0}^M \upphi_{c,t}=1$ gives $\sum_{c=0}^M \psi_{c,t}=0$, so the currency overlay is a zero-investment portfolio. Substituting $\bm{\upphi}_t=\bm{w}_t-\bm{\psi}_t$ into \eqref{eq:hedged-vector} expresses the hedged return in terms of net exposures,
\begin{align}
R^{h}_{\mathcal{P},t+1}
&= \bm{x}_t^\T \bm{R}^{u}_{t+1} + (\bm{w}_t-\bm{\psi}_t)^\T(\bm{f}_t-\bm{e}_{t+1})\nonumber \\[2pt]
&= \bm{x}_t^\T \bm{R}^{u}_{t+1} + \bm{w}_t^\T(\bm{f}_t-\bm{e}_{t+1}) + \bm{\psi}_t^\T(\bm{e}_{t+1}-\bm{f}_t) \nonumber\\[2pt]
&= \bm{x}_t^\T \bm{R}^{fh}_{t+1} + \bm{\psi}_t^\T(\bm{e}_{t+1}-\bm{f}_t),
\label{eq:hedged-net}
\end{align}

which we adopt throughout the following portfolio optimization formulations, as it makes the asset weights $\bm{x}_t$ and the net currency exposures $\bm{\psi}_t$ the explicit variables of the optimization.

\section{Mean-Variance Optimization Formulation} \label{sec:mv-formulation}

\subsection{The Mean-Variance Criterion}              \label{subsec:mv-criterion}
The mean-variance criterion of \cite{Markowitz1952} measures portfolio risk by the
variance of the portfolio return and selects weights that balance this risk against
expected return. Using the hedged portfolio return $R^h_{\mathcal{P},t+1}$ defined in
\eqref{eq:hedged-net} and a relative risk-aversion parameter $\gamma > 0$, the investor
solves
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mv-objective}
\min \;\; \frac{\gamma}{2}\,\mathrm{Var}\!\left(R^h_{\mathcal{P},t+1}\right)
- \mathbb{E}\!\left[R^h_{\mathcal{P},t+1}\right],
\qquad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{1}_N^\top \bm{x}_t = 1.
\end{equation}
The parameter $\gamma$ governs the trade-off between variance and expected return,
larger values place more weight on risk reduction, and in the limit $\gamma \to \infty$
the criterion reduces to pure variance minimization.

The hedged portfolio return \eqref{eq:hedged-net} is linear in the asset weights
$\bm{x}_t$ and the net currency exposures $\bm{\psi}_t$. Consequently
$\mathbb{E}[R^h_{\mathcal{P},t+1}]$ is linear and
$\mathrm{Var}(R^h_{\mathcal{P},t+1})$ is a quadratic form in these decision variables,
so that, once the expected-return vector $\bm{\mu}$ and the covariance matrix
$\bm{\Sigma}$ are estimated, \eqref{eq:mv-objective} is a convex quadratic program.
Whether the asset and currency weights are chosen sequentially or in a single step
determines the precise decision vector and the accompanying constraints; these two 
formulations are developed in Sections~\ref{subsec:mv-overlay}
and~\ref{subsec:mv-joint}.

While analytically convenient, variance is not a coherent risk measure in the sense of 
\cite{CoherentMeasuresOfRisk}, who characterize a coherent measure by monotonicity,
subadditivity, positive homogeneity, and translation invariance. Variance violates
monotonicity: a portfolio whose return dominates another in every state of the world
need not have lower variance, so variance can rank a uniformly preferable position as
the riskier one. Equally relevant for international portfolios exposed to regime shifts
and market stress, variance penalizes upside and downside deviations symmetrically and
therefore does not isolate the large losses that most concern investors. These
shortcomings motivate the coherent, downside-focused alternative developed in
Section~\ref{sec:es-formulation}, where Expected Shortfall replaces variance as the risk
measure while the surrounding optimization structure is left unchanged.
