\emph{Draft outline; bullets to be replaced by prose.}

This chapter asks whether joint optimization of assets and currencies is worth the additional complexity 
rather than just doingsequential currency overlay. First we establish that the joint program's
in-sample advantage is guaranteed by construction, since the overlay's solution
is feasible for it, we show this across the five risk measures. We further show that the advantage largely fails to survive out of
sample: it is significant only in the smallest universe, and the share of it
that is realized falls as the in-sample advantage grows. We
identify the reason for that inversion, namely that the in-sample advantage is
inflated by estimation error in proportion to how little of the sample the risk
measure uses. The central evidence is a sweep of Expected Shortfall over
fourteen confidence levels, which varies the number of observations entering the
objective by a factor of sixteen while holding the risk measure, the universe
and the data fixed; it is corroborated by a comparison across measures that
separates those using the whole sample from those using only its tail. Neither
regularization nor the addition of a mean term alters the conclusion. What
follows is that the in-sample gap is a measure of how much room the joint
program has to fit noise rather than of its economic value, so that joint
optimization is justified in small universes and least of all for the tail
measures whose in-sample case appears strongest.


\section{The In-Sample Case for Joint Optimization}\label{sec:joint-in-sample-case}

Write the decision vector as $\bm{\theta} = (\bm{x}, \bm{\uppsi})$ and let $\Theta$ denote the feasible set defined by the budget and leverage constraints of \Cref{chap:optimization-framework}. Let $\hat f$ be the empirical risk on the estimation window and $f^{oos}$ the realized risk on the holding quarter. The two programs are
\begin{equation}
\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J = \argmin_{\bm{\theta} \in \Theta} \hat f(\bm{\theta}),
\qquad
\hat{\bm{x}}_O = \argmin_{\bm{x}} \hat f_A(\bm{x}),
\quad
\hat{\bm{\uppsi}}_O = \argmin_{\bm{\uppsi}} \hat f(\hat{\bm{x}}_O, \bm{\uppsi}),
\label{eq:two-programs}
\end{equation}
where $\hat f_A$ is the asset-only risk, which ignores the currency block, and $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O = (\hat{\bm{x}}_O, \hat{\bm{\uppsi}}_O)$. The sequential program is a restricted minimizer: it chooses $\bm{x}$ against a different objective, then chooses $\bm{\uppsi}$ with $\hat{\bm{x}}_O$ held fixed.

Its output nevertheless satisfies the same constraints, so $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O \in \Theta$, and its second step is a partial minimization of the same $\hat f$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J)
= \min_{\bm{x}, \bm{\uppsi}} \hat f(\bm{x}, \bm{\uppsi})
\;\leq\; \min_{\bm{\uppsi}} \hat f(\hat{\bm{x}}_O, \bm{\uppsi})
= \hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O).
\label{eq:nesting}
\end{equation}
Defining the gap so that a positive value means the joint program is better,
\begin{equation}
g^{is} \coloneqq \hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O) - \hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J) \;\geq\; 0 .
\label{eq:gap-in-sample}
\end{equation}
The in-sample gap is therefore non-negative by construction, and measures what the joint formulation is worth before any estimation error is introduced.

To see what becomes of it out of sample, define the generalization gap of a fitted $\hat{\bm{\theta}}$ as $\epsilon(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) = f^{oos}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}) - \hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}})$. Writing the realized gap $g^{oos} \coloneqq f^{oos}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O) - f^{oos}(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J)$ in these terms and rearranging,
\begin{equation}
g^{oos}
= \underbrace{\big[\hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O) - \hat f(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J)\big]}_{g^{is} \,\geq\, 0}
\;-\; \underbrace{\big[\epsilon(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J) - \epsilon(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O)\big]}_{\Delta\epsilon} .
\label{eq:gap-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The second term is non-negative in expectation. The estimator $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J$ is selected by minimizing over a strictly larger set than $\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O$, so it fits more of the estimation noise, giving $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_J)] \geq \mathbb{E}[\epsilon(\hat{\bm{\theta}}_O)]$ and hence $\mathbb{E}[\Delta\epsilon] \geq 0$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}\big[g^{oos}\big] = g^{is} - \mathbb{E}[\Delta\epsilon] \;\leq\; g^{is} .
\label{eq:gap-bound}
\end{equation}
The in-sample advantage bounds the realizable advantage from above: the larger parameter set that lets the joint program win in sample is the same freedom that costs it out of sample. \Cref{eq:gap-decomposition} is the organising identity of this chapter, and everything that follows is a measurement of $\Delta\epsilon$.

Two conditions are worth making explicit, since both are used later. The argument requires that the quantity being scored is the objective being minimized, which fails for the penalized and mean-bearing programs of \Cref{sec:what-does-not-repair}. It also requires that both programs minimize the \emph{same} $\hat f$, which fails for mean-variance: the joint program applies Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage to the stacked asset-currency covariance matrix, while the sequential program shrinks the asset and currency blocks separately and leaves the cross-covariance unshrunk, so the two quadratic forms differ. Mean-variance is the only measure considered here whose programs pass through a covariance estimator at all; the remaining four are scenario-based and evaluate the same empirical functional on both sides.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Empirical check of \eqref{eq:gap-in-sample}: violated on $8$ of $1365$ unregularized date-cells ($0.6\%$), all of them mean-variance and all at magnitude $\sim 10^{-6}$ against typical gaps of $\sim 10^{-4}$. Consistent with the estimator discrepancy above and with solver tolerance; ES, MAD, SMV and SOES are at $0.0\%$. Doubles as a correctness check.
  \item In-sample gap by measure: SOES $7.23\%$, ES $3.71\%$, SMV $2.13\%$, MAD $1.91\%$, MV $1.45\%$.
  \item State the expectation this creates---tail measures look most promising---since \Cref{sec:what-survives} overturns it.
  \item Figure: the in-sample (blue) series of \Cref{fig:gap-measures}, introduced here and returned to in \Cref{sec:what-survives} for the out-of-sample series.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mechanism: The Information Budget}\label{sec:mechanism}

The joint program estimates $N + K$ parameters; what varies below is how many observations the objective uses to do it. The two tests below vary that quantity in different ways: the first within a single risk measure, the second across measures.

\subsection{Across Tail Depth Within One Risk Measure}\label{sec:mechanism-level}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Cleanest test: measure, universe, constraints and data fixed; only the fraction of the sample used varies. ES over $14$ levels; tail observations $1044 \to 65$, weights unchanged.
  \item In-sample slope per percentage point: $+0.00162$, $+0.00100$, $+0.00060$, all clear of zero. Out-of-sample: $+0.00005$, $+0.00016$, $+0.00037$, all spanning zero.
  \item At top-$10$ the in-sample gap runs $3.11\% \to 10.59\%$ while the realized gap stays at $2.2$--$2.8\%$.
  \item Conclusion: a level-independent structural component, which survives, plus a level-increasing estimation component, which does not.
  \item Report both horizons. The quarterly tail thins to $\sim 2$ observations at level $97.5$; the annual horizon agrees with the production backtests where the quarterly one does not.
\end{itemize}

\section{What Does Not Repair It}\label{sec:what-does-not-repair}

\subsection{Regularization}\label{sec:no-repair-regularization}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The in-sample framework does not extend to penalised programs: joint and overlay minimise $\text{Risk} + \text{penalty}$ with per-date penalties that differ, while scored on a third quantity.
  \item Not hypothetical: the ordering is violated on $30.0\%$ of regularized date-cells against $0.6\%$ unregularized. Justifies scoping the chapter to the unregularized family.
  \item Answered out of sample instead. ES at level $80$, relative reduction in realized ES: $+3.00\%$, $+1.76\%$, $+0.15\%$ unregularized against $+2.01\%$, $+1.40\%$, $-0.21\%$ regularized.
  \item Same pattern, same cut-off: regularization does not change which program realizes less risk, nor the universe size at which the advantage disappears.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{A Mean Term in the Objective}\label{sec:no-repair-mean}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Helps the deviation measures, does little for ES and SOES, which carry the mean implicitly.
  \item Consistent with the mechanism: measures with information to spare can afford a mean, information-starved ones cannot.
  \item The nesting proposition fails here too, since risk aversion is selected per date. Rely on realized quantities.
\end{itemize}

\section{When Joint Optimization Is Justified}\label{sec:when-justified}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Small universes, variance-based measures; not the tail measures that look best in sample. Counterintuitive, and it follows from the mechanism: their in-sample appeal is their estimation problem.
  \item Net of costs, at top-$10$: volatility $-2.5$ to $-5.7\%$, Expected Shortfall $-1.8$ to $-3.9\%$, no mean effect. At top-$100$ nothing significant.
  \item Cross-reference \Cref{app:sharpe_test} rather than repeating \Cref{sec:what-survives}.
  \item The honest limit: at $700$--$1400$ securities against $14$ currencies the two are indistinguishable out of sample, and the overlay is cheaper.
\end{itemize}
