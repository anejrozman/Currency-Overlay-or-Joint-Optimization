\begin{abstract}
Investing internationally exposes a portfolio to exchange rate risk. A common way to manage this risk is a currency
overlay, in which the investor first chooses the asset portfolio and then adds a separate set of currency positions to
hedge or adjust its currency exposure. This thesis studies whether optimizing the asset and currency positions jointly
improves on the sequential currency overlay out of sample, and under which conditions. We compare the two approaches
under five risk measures, variance, semi-variance, mean absolute deviation, Expected Shortfall, and second-order
Expected Shortfall. We consider three formulations, unregularized risk minimization, regularized risk minimization,
and a mean-risk formulation that also rewards expected returns. We backtest each of them in three investable universes
of increasing size. When the optimizers only minimize risk, with or without regularization, the joint and overlay
optimizers earn very similar risk-adjusted returns. Joint optimization attains lower risk in sample, but out of sample
this advantage remains only in the smallest universe, where currencies make up a larger share of the decision
variables. Under Expected Shortfall and second-order Expected Shortfall, the in-sample advantage is larger. It is
driven by two mechanisms. The joint program fits the currency positions to the worst past days and favors portfolios
that happened to earn high returns. Neither mechanism generalizes out of sample. Both approaches manage the currencies
actively. Compared with full hedging, this lowers risk at similar risk-adjusted returns, and in the larger universes
it also outperforms leaving the currencies unhedged. In the mean-risk formulation, by contrast, the joint optimizers
achieve higher Sharpe ratios than the overlay optimizers for every risk measure in the larger universes, because they
can weigh the expected returns of assets and currencies against each other. These results indicate that, for pure risk
minimization, joint optimization is beneficial in small investable universes, although the benefit decreases as the
universe grows, and that it is preferable when expected returns are part of the objective.
\end{abstract}
