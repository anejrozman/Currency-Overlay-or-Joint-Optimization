\begin{abstract}
    Multi-currency portfolio construction requires managing asset and currency exposures together, yet it 
    remains unclear whether these decisions are best made simultaneously or sequentially as the investable universe grows. 
    This thesis builds on the regularized multi-currency Expected Shortfall framework of Lucescu and Ulrych (2026), 
    in which portfolios are formed by minimizing a risk objective subject to $L^1$ and $L^2$ penalties, with currency 
    exposures either optimized jointly with assets or handled through a separate overlay. 
    First we replicate the framework's core strategies, then extend it along two dimensions,
    the size of the investable universe, and the choice of objective function, implementing both Expected Shortfall 
    and mean-variance formulations of the joint and overlay optimizers.
    
    Across both objectives, the joint optimizer's out-of-sample performance 
    deteriorates relative to the separate overlay as the universe expands. 
    This motivates the central question of the thesis, is this deterioration a structural 
    property of joint optimization, or is it modulated by the choice of objective function? 
    To answer it, we develop a general regularized optimization framework that admits arbitrary 
    objective functions within a single, common formulation. We validate the framework by showing that 
    it exactly reproduces the existing Expected Shortfall and mean-variance optimizers, and then use it 
    to measure how the rate of the joint optimizer's breakdown varies as dimensionality increases.

\end{abstract}
